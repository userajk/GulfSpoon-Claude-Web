What Can You Cook in an Air Fryer? Foods That Work, and What to Avoid

You can cook almost anything in an air fryer that would roast, grill or oven-fry well in small portions: chicken pieces, steak bites, salmon, potatoes, most vegetables, eggs, frozen snacks, small bakes and leftovers you want crisp again. What does not work is anything that needs liquid to cook, a wet batter to set in hot oil, or more space than the basket has, and this guide covers both lists with times and temperatures to start from.

The Short List: What Air Fryers Do Best

An air fryer is a small, fast convection oven. It does its best work on food that is cut or portioned small enough to sit in a single layer and that benefits from a dry, browned surface. That covers most of what people cook on a weeknight.

The table gives a starting temperature and time range for each category in a 5 to 6 litre basket air fryer, preheated. Recipes for individual foods give exact times.

Category Good examples Typical temperature Typical time Doneness check
Chicken pieces Thighs, wings, tenders, cutlets 180–220°C (350–425°F) 8–28 min 74°C (165°F) in the thickest part
Beef and lamb Steak bites, kofta, burgers, halal sausages 190–200°C (375–400°F) 6–15 min Mince and sausages 71°C (160°F); whole cuts at least 63°C (145°F) plus rest
Fish and seafood Salmon fillets, salmon bites, prawns, breaded fish 180–200°C (350–400°F) 6–12 min 63°C (145°F), flesh opaque and flaking
Potatoes Cubes, wedges, baby potatoes, whole jacket potatoes 200°C (400°F) 15–45 min Crisp outside, knife slides through
Vegetables Cauliflower, broccoli, mushrooms, asparagus, peppers 180–200°C (350–400°F) 6–18 min Charred edges, tender stems
Eggs Boiled eggs in the shell, frittata, quiche in a dish 135–160°C (275–320°F) 9–28 min Boiled: follow the chart for yolk texture; quiche and frittata 71°C (160°F) in the centre
Frozen snacks Fries, nuggets, samosas, spring rolls 180–200°C (350–400°F) 8–20 min Hot through, crisp shell
Baking Cookies, muffins, small cakes 150–165°C (300–325°F) 7–25 min Skewer comes out clean
Reheating Pizza, fried chicken, samosas, roast potatoes 160–180°C (325–350°F) 3–8 min Steaming hot in the centre

Baking temperatures are lower than you might expect because the element sits so close to the food. A cake at oven temperature sets hard on top before the middle rises.

Chicken

Chicken is the food air fryers handle best. Skin-on pieces render their own fat into the drawer and the skin crisps without added oil, and breaded pieces come out with a crunch close to fried.

  • Thighs. Bone-in, skin-on thighs take 20 to 22 minutes at 200°C (400°F) and boneless ones 15 to 18. Thigh meat is forgiving because the extra fat keeps it juicy. Our air fryer chicken thighs recipe uses a garlic and paprika rub that suits both.
  • Wings. Pat dry, toss with baking powder and salt, and they crisp in 24 to 28 minutes. The crispy chicken wings recipe starts them at 180°C (360°F) to render the fat, then finishes at 220°C (425°F).
  • Tenders and cutlets. Breaded strips take 12 to 14 minutes at 200°C (400°F). For something quicker, thin-sliced chicken breast cooks in under 10 minutes and stays juicy if you pull it at 74°C (165°F).
  • Chicken parmesan. A breaded breast crisps in the basket, then gets a spoon of homemade marinara and mozzarella for the last few minutes, so the cheese melts before the crumb underneath goes soft. Our chicken parm gives the full method.
  • Marinated chicken for wraps. Shawarma-spiced or tikka-marinated thigh pieces brown well. Shake off excess marinade first, since loose yoghurt or oil drips and smokes.

What does not fit: a whole chicken over about 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) usually touches the element in a 5 to 6 litre basket. Spatchcock it or use the oven.

Beef, Lamb and Halal Sausages

Red meat works when it is cut small or shaped flat. The air fryer gives a good crust on cubes and patties, but it cannot match a smoking-hot pan for a thick steak's sear.

  • Steak bites. Cubes of sirloin, 2.5 cm across, take 6 to 8 minutes at 200°C (400°F) for medium, 63°C (145°F) inside, then a 3-minute rest. The garlic butter steak bites recipe has a doneness chart.
  • Burgers and kofta. Halal beef or lamb patties 2 cm thick take 10 to 14 minutes, flipped halfway, to reach 71°C (160°F). Kofta on short skewers take 10 to 12 minutes and need the same 71°C.
  • Halal sausages and bratwurst. Halal beef or chicken sausages cook evenly and do not split the way they often do in a pan. Our halal beef brats cook with sliced peppers and onions in the same basket.
  • Halal turkey bacon. Lies flat and crisps in 7 to 9 minutes at 180°C (360°F), or stays chewy at 5 to 6, without spitting fat across the hob. Turkey bacon in the air fryer covers chewy versus crisp timings.
  • Lamb chops. Thin loin chops take 8 to 12 minutes at 200°C (400°F) to reach at least 63°C (145°F), then rest for 3 minutes. Trim thick fat caps first; lamb fat dripping into the drawer is the commonest cause of white smoke.

Fish and Seafood

Fish cooks quickly and evenly in an air fryer, and there is no smell of hot oil hanging in the kitchen afterwards.

  • Salmon fillets. A 2.5 cm thick fillet takes 8 to 10 minutes at 200°C (400°F). The air fryer salmon recipe covers skin-on and skinless.
  • Salmon bites. Cubed salmon glazed with honey and garlic or chilli takes 6 to 8 minutes. Salmon bites are a good way to stretch one large fillet across four people.
  • Salmon straight from the freezer. No thawing needed. Frozen salmon in the air fryer gives times by thickness.
  • Prawns. Raw, peeled prawns take 5 to 7 minutes at 200°C. Breaded frozen prawns take 8 to 10.
  • White fish. Hammour, cod or seabream fillets work best with a coating of breadcrumbs or a spice crust, because lean white fish dries out faster than salmon. Cook to 63°C (145°F).

Potatoes, Vegetables and Pulses

Potatoes crisp well in hot moving air. Cubes, wedges and whole jacket potatoes all work, and each has its own method.

  • Cubed potatoes. 2.5 cm cubes with a tablespoon of oil take 18 to 22 minutes at 200°C (400°F). See air fryer potatoes for russet versus waxy varieties.
  • Baby potatoes. Halved, they crisp on the cut face in about 15 to 18 minutes. Baby potatoes also covers cooking them whole.
  • Jacket potatoes. A medium potato takes about 35 minutes at 200°C (400°F) and a large one 40 to 45, with skin that crackles. The baked potato recipe has the method.
  • Sweet potatoes. Wedges, fries and cubed sweet potato all brown well because of their natural sugar. Watch them in the last few minutes.

Most vegetables do well too, as long as they are dry and lightly oiled. Cauliflower, mushrooms and asparagus each have a dedicated recipe, and our roundup of vegetables in the air fryer gives a time chart for broccoli, carrots, courgette, okra, peppers and more.

Pulses and plant proteins: tinned chickpeas turn crunchy once they are dried thoroughly before cooking (see crispy roasted chickpeas), and pressed tofu cubes go crisp in 12 to 15 minutes with a cornflour coating.

Eggs and Breakfast

Breakfast is where the air fryer saves most washing up, because it replaces the grill, the frying pan and the toaster for small portions.

  • Boiled eggs in the shell. No water needed. At 135°C (275°F), fridge-cold eggs take 15 to 17 minutes for hard boiled, a few minutes longer than a pan of water. Models vary, so start with our boiled eggs chart and adjust by a minute.
  • Quiche and egg cups. A crustless quiche in an 18 to 20 cm dish takes 22 to 28 minutes at 160°C (320°F), and ramekins 12 to 15. Air fryer quiche covers crustless, pastry and mini versions with halal fillings.
  • Shakshuka-style eggs. Warm a tomato and pepper sauce in a small heatproof dish, crack eggs into it and cook at 180°C (350°F) for 6 to 9 minutes, until the whites are fully set; give it longer if you want firm yolks. For the full stovetop version, see Gulf shakshuka.
  • Toast and garlic bread. Toast works but light slices can lift in the fan, so weigh them with a trivet. Garlic toast is a better use of the basket.
  • Frozen breakfast staples. Waffles, hash brown patties and parathas all crisp from frozen. Hash brown patties go in straight from the freezer.

Frozen Foods and Ramadan Snacks

Frozen food is where an air fryer earns its counter space. Par-fried products such as fries, nuggets, spring rolls and samosas were designed to be finished in hot air or oil, and the air fryer gets them crisper than an oven in less time.

During Ramadan this matters. Many Gulf households keep bags of frozen samosas, spring rolls, cheese sambousek and kibbeh for iftar, and frying several batches every evening fills the kitchen with oil smell and heat. An air fryer handles a plate of them in 10 to 15 minutes at 180 to 200°C (350 to 400°F), with a light mist of oil on any that look dry.

A few rules for frozen food:

  • Do not thaw breaded or pastry items first. They go soggy and the coating slides off.
  • Check whether it is pre-cooked. Nuggets and most spring rolls are fully cooked and only need reheating. Some breaded chicken and some frozen kibbeh contain raw meat and need a thermometer check.
  • Halal certification. In the Gulf, most frozen meat products carry it; abroad, check the pack for a recognised halal mark on nuggets, burgers, meatballs and sausages.

For times on fries, nuggets, waffles, lasagna portions, parathas and more, use our frozen food time and temperature chart, which also explains how to convert oven instructions on the box. Taquitos, homemade or frozen, have their own page.

Can You Bake in an Air Fryer?

Yes, in small quantities. Anything that fits in a 15 to 18 cm tin or a few silicone cups will bake, but drop the temperature by 10 to 20°C (20 to 35°F) from the oven recipe and check early.

  • Cookies. Four to six at a time on parchment, 7 to 8 minutes at 165°C (325°F), pulled while the centres still look soft. Our chocolate chip cookies use alcohol-free vanilla.
  • Muffins and cupcakes. In silicone or foil cases, 12 to 15 minutes at 160°C.
  • Small cakes and brownies. A 15 cm tin takes 20 to 25 minutes at 150 to 160°C. Cover loosely with foil if the top darkens before the centre is set.
  • Pastry. Puff pastry twists, cheese and za'atar rolls and small fruit turnovers puff well because the hot air hits them immediately.
  • Bread. Small rolls and flatbreads work. A full loaf is too tall for most baskets.

Reheating Leftovers

Reheating is the use most owners underrate. Anything that was crisp when fresh and goes soft in the microwave comes back to life at 160 to 180°C (325 to 350°F) for 3 to 8 minutes.

  • Pizza: 3 to 4 minutes at 180°C. The base crisps and the cheese re-melts.
  • Fried chicken and breaded cutlets: 5 to 6 minutes, turned once.
  • Samosas, spring rolls and sambousek from last night's iftar: 4 to 5 minutes.
  • Roast potatoes and chips: 4 to 6 minutes, shaken once.
  • Rice dishes such as machboos: reheat in a covered heatproof dish with a splash of water, 8 to 10 minutes at 160°C, stirring halfway. Uncovered rice dries out.

Reheat leftovers until steaming hot all the way through, 74°C (165°F) if you check with a thermometer.

What Not to Put in an Air Fryer

Some foods fail in an air fryer, and a few are unsafe. These are the ones to avoid, or to cook in a different way.

  1. Wet batters. Tempura, fish-and-chip style batter, luqaimat dough and thin pakora batter drip through the basket before they set, because air cannot seal batter the way hot oil does. Use a breadcrumb or dry flour coating instead.
  2. Loose leafy greens. Raw spinach and lettuce are light enough for the fan to lift into the element, where they scorch. Kale works only if well oiled and packed under a trivet.
  3. Raw rice, pasta and grains. They need to absorb water, and an air fryer is a dry-heat appliance. Cook them on the hob and use the air fryer for the toppings.
  4. Large roasts. A whole leg of lamb or a big beef joint will not fit, and if it does, the outside overcooks before the centre is safe. Cut it into portions or use the oven.
  5. Cheese on its own. Halloumi slices and breaded mozzarella sticks work. Unbreaded soft cheese melts through the basket.
  6. Soupy or saucy dishes in the basket. Sauces drip into the drawer and burn. Use a heatproof dish that leaves a gap around the edge for air.
  7. Popcorn. The kernels are light enough to blow around the chamber, and many fail to pop. Use a pan on the hob.
  8. Loose parchment, foil or paper. Never put an unweighted liner in the basket, and never preheat with one. Food must hold it down.
  9. Raw, frozen, stuffed breaded chicken breasts. The USDA says these should not be cooked in an air fryer. Follow the oven instructions on the pack and check with a thermometer that the chicken and filling both reach 74°C (165°F).
  10. Packaging from ready meals. Plastic and cardboard trays are made for microwaves or ovens. Move the food to a heatproof dish that fits.

Very fatty food deserves a mention too. It works, but a full basket of fatty lamb or chicken skin can make the drawer smoke. Pour off the fat between batches and keep the drawer clean; our cleaning guide shows how.

Best Foods to Start With as a Beginner

If you have just unboxed one, cook these in roughly this order. Each teaches you something about how your particular model behaves.

  1. Frozen fries. Learn how much fits before the batch stops crisping, and how often to shake.
  2. Cubed potatoes. Learn what a tablespoon of oil does compared with none.
  3. Chicken wings or thighs. Learn how fat renders and how to check doneness with a thermometer.
  4. Salmon fillet. Learn how quickly delicate food cooks; a fillet needs no flipping.
  5. Boiled eggs. Learn whether your model runs hot or cool against a recipe's timing.
  6. Roasted chickpeas. Learn how dry food needs to be before it goes in.
  7. Reheated pizza or samosas. Learn the reheat setting you will use most.

Most beginner disappointments come from overfilling the basket. Our explainer on how air fryers work shows why airflow matters more than anything else.

Can You Cook a Full Dinner in an Air Fryer?

Yes, for two to three people, if you plan around one protein and one side and start the slower one first.

  • Chicken thighs and potatoes. Start 2.5 cm potato cubes at 200°C (400°F). After 4 minutes, lay boneless thighs on top and cook 15 to 18 minutes more, until the thighs read 74°C (165°F). Lift the chicken out to rest and give the potatoes a shake and 2 to 3 minutes on their own; the ones underneath need it.
  • Salmon and asparagus. Put the salmon in, then add asparagus spears tossed in oil for the last 6 minutes.
  • Steak bites and mushrooms. Mushrooms first for 5 minutes to release their water, then steak bites on top for 6 to 8 minutes.
  • Halal brats with peppers and onions. The peppers and onions get a 5-minute head start, then the brats go on top and everything finishes together.
  • Nachos for sharing. Layer tortilla chips, seasoned halal beef or chicken and cheese in a foil tray or on a liner, 4 to 5 minutes at 180°C (350°F). Build them in small batches so the bottom chips stay crisp; loaded nachos has the method.

A dual-zone model makes this simpler: cook each part in its own drawer and use the sync setting so both finish together. For six or more people, the oven is still the better tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can you put in an air fryer?

You can put any food that roasts or oven-fries well in small portions: chicken pieces, beef cubes, burgers, fish fillets, potatoes, most vegetables, eggs, frozen snacks and small bakes. Use heatproof dishes, silicone moulds and perforated liners that food holds down. Avoid plastic or cardboard packaging and loose paper or foil.

What should you not cook in an air fryer?

Avoid wet batters, which drip before they set, loose leafy greens that blow into the element, raw rice and pasta, large roasts, unbreaded soft cheese, popcorn and saucy dishes straight in the basket. Raw stuffed breaded chicken breasts belong in the oven, following the pack, not in the air fryer.

What is the best thing to cook first in a new air fryer?

Frozen fries are the best first cook. They are cheap, forgiving and show you how much your basket holds before food stops crisping and how often to shake. After that, try cubed potatoes, chicken wings and a salmon fillet to learn how fat, oil and thickness change timing.

Can you cook raw meat in an air fryer?

Yes. Raw chicken, beef, lamb and fish all cook safely in an air fryer as long as they reach a safe internal temperature: 74°C (165°F) for chicken, 71°C (160°F) for mince and sausages, 63°C (145°F) for fish, and at least 63°C (145°F) for whole cuts of beef or lamb followed by a 3-minute rest. Use a probe thermometer.

Can you put foil or parchment paper in an air fryer?

Yes, if food weighs it down and air can still circulate. Use perforated parchment or a small piece of foil under the food, leaving space around the edges. Never preheat with a loose liner in the basket, because the fan can lift it into the heating element.

Can you cook rice in an air fryer?

Not from raw in the basket. Rice needs to absorb water, and an air fryer is a dry-heat appliance. Cook rice on the hob or in a rice cooker. You can reheat cooked rice in a covered heatproof dish with a splash of water at 160°C for 8 to 10 minutes.

Can you bake a cake in an air fryer?

Yes, a small one. Use a 15 to 18 cm tin that leaves a gap around the edge, set the air fryer 10 to 20°C lower than the oven recipe, usually 150 to 160°C, and bake for 20 to 25 minutes. Cover with foil if the top browns before a skewer comes out clean.

Is it worth cooking dinner for a family in an air fryer?

For two to four people, yes, if you cook in batches or use a dual-zone model. A 5 to 6 litre basket holds about two to three portions of protein at once. For six or more, an oven usually finishes faster overall because everything cooks in one go.
