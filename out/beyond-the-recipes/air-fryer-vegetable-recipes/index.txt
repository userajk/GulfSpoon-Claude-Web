34:T4181,<h2 id="method">How to cook vegetables in an air fryer</h2>
<p>An air fryer is a small, powerful fan oven. Hot air moving fast over the surface of a vegetable dries it, and once the surface is dry it browns. Almost every rule below comes back to that: anything that keeps water on the surface, or traps steam around the food, gives you soft, pale vegetables. If you want the mechanics in more detail, our explainer on <a href="/beyond-the-recipes/how-does-an-air-fryer-work/">how an air fryer works</a> covers the fan, the element and why shaking matters.</p>
<p>The method that works for nearly every vegetable:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Cut evenly.</strong> Pieces of the same size cook at the same rate. For most vegetables, aim for 2 to 3cm (about 1 inch) pieces or 1cm-thick batons.</li>
<li><strong>Dry thoroughly.</strong> Spin or pat washed vegetables dry. Surface water has to evaporate before browning can start.</li>
<li><strong>Oil lightly.</strong> Use about 1 tablespoon of oil per 450g (1 lb). It conducts heat into the surface and helps seasoning stick. More than that drips into the drawer and smokes.</li>
<li><strong>Preheat.</strong> Three minutes at 200°C (400°F) is enough for most basket models. With cook times under 10 minutes, a cold start throws the timing off.</li>
<li><strong>Single layer.</strong> A 5 to 6 litre basket takes about 450g of most vegetables in one loose layer. Pile them deeper and the bottom layer steams.</li>
<li><strong>Shake halfway.</strong> This turns the pieces and moves the ones from the centre to the hotter edges.</li>
<li><strong>Salt at the right time.</strong> For dense vegetables (carrots, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, potatoes) salt before cooking. For watery ones (courgette, mushrooms, aubergine, tomatoes) salt after cooking, because salt draws out water that then steams them.</li>
</ol>
<p>Doneness looks the same across most vegetables: browned edges, a centre a knife slides into with little resistance, and no liquid pooling in the drawer.</p>
<h2 id="chart">Air fryer vegetable cooking times chart</h2>
<p>All times are for a preheated 5 to 6 litre basket air fryer with about 450g (1 lb) of vegetables in one layer. For a compact 3 to 4 litre basket, cook half the amount and check 1 to 2 minutes early. For an oven-style air fryer with racks, add 2 to 3 minutes and swap rack positions halfway.</p>

<h2 id="recipes">Quick air fryer vegetable recipes</h2>
<p>Each of these serves 2 to 3 as a side and starts with about 450g (1 lb) of vegetables, 1 tablespoon of oil and ½ teaspoon of salt unless stated. Follow the chart for time and temperature.</p>
<h3>Broccoli with lemon and chilli</h3>
<p>Cut florets to 3 to 4cm and slice the stalk into 1cm coins; peeled stalk is sweet and cooks in the same time. Toss with oil, salt and ½ teaspoon of chilli flakes. Small florets char fast at the tips, which is the point, but anything smaller than 2cm will burn, so keep pieces even. Finish with lemon zest and a squeeze of juice. Done when the floret tops are dark brown at the edges and the stalks are tender.</p>
<h3>Honey cumin carrots</h3>
<p>Cut carrots into 1cm batons. Toss with oil, salt and ½ teaspoon of ground cumin and cook 12 minutes, then drizzle with 2 teaspoons of honey or date syrup, shake and cook 2 to 4 minutes more. Adding the honey at the start burns it before the carrots soften. Done when a knife slides through the thickest baton and the edges are caramelised.</p>
<h3>Courgette with za&#39;atar</h3>
<p>Cut courgettes into 1.5cm half-moons. Toss with oil only and cook 10 to 12 minutes, shaking once. Season with salt and 1 teaspoon of za&#39;atar after cooking, while still hot. Courgette is mostly water, and salting early leaves it limp. Done when the cut faces are golden and the flesh is tender but still holding its shape.</p>
<h3>Garlic green beans</h3>
<p>Trim the stem ends and toss the beans with oil and salt. Cook 8 to 10 minutes, shaking once, until blistered in spots. Meanwhile, warm 1 tablespoon of olive oil with 1 grated garlic clove, then pour over the hot beans with a few toasted flaked almonds. Done when the beans are wrinkled in places and bend without snapping.</p>
<h3>Crispy Brussels sprouts</h3>
<p>Trim the bases and halve the sprouts, keeping loose outer leaves (they turn into crisp flakes). Toss with oil and salt and cook at 190°C (375°F) for 14 to 18 minutes, shaking twice. The lower temperature lets the dense centres cook before the leaves burn. Finish with ½ teaspoon of sumac or a drizzle of pomegranate molasses. Done when a knife slides into the centre of the largest half.</p>
<h3>Charred peppers</h3>
<p>Use red, yellow or orange peppers; green ones taste more bitter once charred. Cut into 2 to 3cm strips, toss with oil, salt and ½ teaspoon of smoked paprika and cook 8 to 10 minutes until blistered with dark spots. They are good in wraps, over hummus or with grilled meat.</p>
<h3>Spiced aubergine</h3>
<p>Cut a medium aubergine (about 350g) into 2.5cm cubes. Aubergine soaks up oil like a sponge, so use 2 tablespoons and toss well so every cube is coated. Add ½ teaspoon each of ground cumin and coriander. Cook 12 to 15 minutes, shaking twice, then salt. Done when the cubes are browned outside and creamy all the way through when pressed. Serve with garlic yoghurt and chopped mint.</p>
<h3>Crispy okra</h3>
<p>Okra (bamia) is easy to find fresh in Gulf markets all year. Rinse and dry it completely, then trim just the stem caps without cutting into the pods, which keeps the slippery juice inside. Toss whole pods with oil, salt and ½ teaspoon of chilli powder or chaat masala and cook 10 to 12 minutes, shaking twice. For crispier okra, halve the pods lengthways, toss in 1 tablespoon of chickpea flour after the oil and cook 8 to 10 minutes. Done when the ridges are browned and the pods are tender.</p>
<h3>Corn on the cob</h3>
<p>Cut cobs in half to fit the basket, brush with oil and cook 12 to 15 minutes, turning once, until the kernels are spotted brown. Brush with butter mixed with chilli powder and lime zest, or with a little tahini and lemon. Shorter frozen half-cobs work too: add 3 to 4 minutes.</p>

<h2 id="mixed">How to roast mixed vegetables in the air fryer</h2>
<p>Mixed vegetables cook unevenly if they all go in together, because a carrot baton needs nearly twice as long as a pepper strip. Start the slowest group, then add the others so everything finishes at once.</p>

<h2 id="frozen">How to cook frozen vegetables in the air fryer</h2>
<p>Cook frozen vegetables straight from the freezer. Thawing them first lets them sit in their own water and they go soggy. Ice melts in the first few minutes, so if water collects in the drawer, pour it off at the first shake.</p>

<h2 id="seasonings">Seasonings and sauces for air fryer vegetables</h2>
<p>Dry spices go on with the oil for dense vegetables. Fresh herbs, citrus, sugar and cheese go on at the end, because they burn at 200°C.</p>
<p><strong>Spice blends (per 450g of vegetables):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Za&#39;atar and sumac:</strong> 1 tsp za&#39;atar and ½ tsp sumac after cooking. Good on courgette, carrots and cauliflower.</li>
<li><strong>Baharat:</strong> 1 tsp with the oil. Good on aubergine, squash and onions.</li>
<li><strong>Chaat masala:</strong> ½ to 1 tsp after cooking. Good on okra, corn and potatoes.</li>
<li><strong>Smoked paprika and garlic:</strong> ½ tsp each, garlic powder added for the last 5 minutes. Good on peppers, broccoli and chickpeas.</li>
<li><strong>Cumin and coriander:</strong> ½ tsp each with the oil. Good on carrots and aubergine.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Sauces to serve alongside:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tahini lemon:</strong> 3 tbsp tahini, juice of ½ lemon, a small grated garlic clove, salt and enough cold water to make it pourable.</li>
<li><strong>Garlic yoghurt:</strong> 150g thick yoghurt or labneh, 1 grated garlic clove, salt and dried mint.</li>
<li><strong>Chilli lime:</strong> 2 tbsp olive oil, zest and juice of 1 lime, ½ tsp chilli flakes and a pinch of salt.</li>
<li><strong>Pomegranate dressing:</strong> 1 tbsp pomegranate molasses whisked with 2 tbsp olive oil and a squeeze of lemon.</li>
</ul>
<p>These all keep for 3 to 4 days in the fridge, so make a jar on the weekend and a tray of vegetables becomes a meal in 15 minutes during the week.</p>

36:T631,Cauliflower is one of the most underrated vegetables for the air fryer. The circulating heat hits every surface of each floret, crisping the edges to a deep golden brown while keeping the interior tender and slightly nutty. In a conventional oven, this level of caramelisation takes 30 to 40 minutes and usually requires flipping. In the air fryer, you get there in 15 minutes with a single shake of the basket.

The garlic and parmesan here are not just seasoning. They are architecture. The parmesan melts into a thin, crispy crust on the surface of each floret, and the garlic toasts to a sweet, mellow flavor that bears no resemblance to the sharp bite of raw garlic. Together, they transform plain cauliflower into something that genuinely competes with fries and chips for snacking appeal.

The trick to great air fryer cauliflower is not overcrowding the basket. Cauliflower releases moisture as it cooks, and if the florets are packed in too tightly, they steam instead of roasting. The difference between crowded cauliflower (soft, pale, slightly mushy) and properly spaced cauliflower (crispy, golden, caramelised) is dramatic.

In Gulf kitchens, cauliflower has always been popular, used in everything from Indian-influenced gobi dishes to Arabic-style fried cauliflower with tahini. The air fryer version adds a Western twist with parmesan while keeping the simplicity that makes cauliflower a weeknight staple.
