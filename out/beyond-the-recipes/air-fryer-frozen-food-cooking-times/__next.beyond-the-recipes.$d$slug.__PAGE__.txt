3a:T41dd,<h2 id="chart">Frozen Food Air Fryer Cooking Times Chart</h2>
<p>These times assume a 5 to 6 litre basket air fryer, preheated for 3 minutes, with food straight from the freezer in a single layer or, for fries and nuggets, no more than half full. Start checking at the low end of each range. Smaller 2 to 3 litre baskets need the same temperature with smaller batches; oven-style air fryers usually need 2 to 4 minutes more.</p>
<p>Pack sizes and thicknesses vary between brands, so treat every time as a starting point and use the visual cue in the notes column.</p>

<h2 id="oven-conversion">How to Convert Oven Instructions on the Pack</h2>
<p>Lower the oven temperature by 15 to 20°C (25 to 35°F), cut the time by 20 to 30%, and check at the halfway point. That covers most frozen food, because an air fryer&#39;s small chamber and strong fan cook faster than an oven at the same setting.</p>
<p>Check which oven the pack means first:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Conventional oven temperature (US packs, and the higher figure on UK and Gulf packs):</strong> subtract 15 to 20°C (25 to 35°F).</li>
<li><strong>Fan oven temperature (often printed alongside):</strong> use it as it is, or 10°C lower. A fan oven already circulates air, so the gap is smaller.</li>
</ul>
<p>A worked example. A bag of frozen samosas says &quot;conventional oven 220°C for 20 to 25 minutes, fan oven 200°C&quot;. In the air fryer, set 190 to 200°C and start checking at 12 minutes, turning them at 6. Most batches finish between 12 and 16 minutes.</p>
<p>Packs that already give air fryer instructions are worth following, since the manufacturer has tested that product. If the pack says &quot;not suitable for air fryer&quot; or gives oven instructions only for a raw, stuffed chicken product, follow the pack.</p>
<h2 id="thaw-preheat">Do You Thaw or Preheat for Frozen Food?</h2>
<p>Do not thaw. Breaded, battered and pastry foods are designed to go from freezer to heat. Thawing lets the coating absorb moisture, so it turns soggy and can slide off in the basket. The exceptions are large items the chart does not cover, such as a whole frozen joint, which should be thawed in the fridge and cooked in the oven.</p>
<p>Do preheat, for 3 to 5 minutes. A hot basket starts setting the coating immediately, before the surface thaws and softens. If you skip preheating, add 2 to 3 minutes and expect slightly paler results.</p>
<h2 id="pre-cooked-or-raw">Is It Pre-Cooked or Raw? Reading the Pack</h2>
<p>Check this on every frozen meat product before you set a time. Two items that look identical in the freezer can need very different handling.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pre-cooked</strong> (&quot;fully cooked&quot;, &quot;heat and serve&quot;, &quot;ready to eat once heated&quot;): most nuggets, chicken strips, meatballs, corn dogs and spring rolls. You are reheating them until hot through, so timing is about texture.</li>
<li><strong>Raw</strong> (&quot;uncooked&quot;, &quot;raw&quot;, &quot;cook thoroughly&quot;, &quot;cook until 74°C/165°F&quot;): some breaded chicken, many burgers, kofta, kibbeh and wings. These must reach a safe internal temperature, checked with a probe thermometer in the thickest part: 74°C (165°F) for chicken, 71°C (160°F) for minced beef or lamb.</li>
</ul>
<p>One product needs special care. The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service says raw, stuffed, breaded chicken breast products (cheese-filled or Kiev-style) should not be cooked in an air fryer, and that you should follow the manufacturer&#39;s cooking instructions. They are browned on the outside but raw inside, so they look done long before they are. Use the oven method on the pack and confirm 74°C (165°F) in both the chicken and the filling.</p>
<h2 id="crisp">How to Get Frozen Food Crisp, Not Soggy</h2>
<p>Soggy frozen food is almost always a water problem. Ice on the surface melts and has to evaporate before the coating can crisp, and a crowded basket traps that steam.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Shake off loose ice</strong> before cooking. Frost from a half-used bag adds a surprising amount of water.</li>
<li><strong>Leave space.</strong> A single layer is best. For fries and nuggets, fill the basket no more than halfway and shake every 5 to 6 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Add oil only where the pack does not.</strong> Par-fried foods such as fries, nuggets and mini spring rolls already contain oil. Unfried pastry, kibbeh and large spring rolls brown far better with a light mist of oil. Parathas already contain plenty of fat and need none.</li>
<li><strong>Pour off water.</strong> Wings and burgers from frozen release liquid into the drawer. Tip it out halfway so it does not steam the food.</li>
<li><strong>Rest briefly.</strong> Two minutes on a rack, not a plate, lets steam escape from the base.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not chase colour too far with potato products. The UK Food Standards Agency advises cooking starchy foods such as chips to a golden yellow colour or lighter, to limit acrylamide.</p>
<h2 id="iftar">Iftar Batches: Samosas and Spring Rolls for a Crowd</h2>
<p>During Ramadan, many Gulf households air fry two or three trays of frozen samosas, spring rolls and sambousek every evening. A 5 to 6 litre basket holds about 10 to 12 medium samosas in a single layer, so plan batches rather than piling them in.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Start the first batch 20 minutes before maghrib.</strong> Keep finished batches warm, uncovered, on a rack in a 100°C (210°F) oven so they stay crisp.</li>
<li><strong>Cook the unfried pastry first.</strong> Thick-pastry samosas take the longest; pre-fried mini spring rolls only need 8 to 10 minutes and can go in last.</li>
<li><strong>Homemade samosas freeze well.</strong> Freeze filled, unbaked samosas on a tray, then bag them. Brush with oil and cook from frozen at 180°C (350°F) for 14 to 18 minutes, turning once. A cooked filling just needs to be hot through; a raw minced meat filling must reach 71°C (160°F).</li>
<li><strong>Reheating leftovers</strong> the next day takes 4 to 5 minutes at 170°C (340°F).</li>
</ul>
