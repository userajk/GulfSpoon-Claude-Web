Frozen Food in the Air Fryer: Cooking Times for Fries, Nuggets, Samosas and More

Most frozen food cooks in an air fryer at 180–200°C (350–400°F) in about 20 to 30% less time than the oven instructions on the pack, shaken or turned halfway: thin fries take 12–16 minutes, chicken nuggets 8–10, fish fingers 8–10 and frozen samosas 12–15. The chart below covers more than 40 freezer staples, from waffles and lasagna portions to kibbeh and parathas, with a method for converting any oven instruction you find on the box.

Frozen Food Air Fryer Cooking Times Chart

These times assume a 5 to 6 litre basket air fryer, preheated for 3 minutes, with food straight from the freezer in a single layer or, for fries and nuggets, no more than half full. Start checking at the low end of each range. Smaller 2 to 3 litre baskets need the same temperature with smaller batches; oven-style air fryers usually need 2 to 4 minutes more.

Pack sizes and thicknesses vary between brands, so treat every time as a starting point and use the visual cue in the notes column.

Choose nuggets, burgers, meatballs and franks with a recognised halal certification mark. In the Gulf most supermarket ranges carry one; abroad, check every pack.

How to Convert Oven Instructions on the Pack

Lower the oven temperature by 15 to 20°C (25 to 35°F), cut the time by 20 to 30%, and check at the halfway point. That covers most frozen food, because an air fryer's small chamber and strong fan cook faster than an oven at the same setting.

Check which oven the pack means first:

  • Conventional oven temperature (US packs, and the higher figure on UK and Gulf packs): subtract 15 to 20°C (25 to 35°F).
  • Fan oven temperature (often printed alongside): use it as it is, or 10°C lower. A fan oven already circulates air, so the gap is smaller.

A worked example. A bag of frozen samosas says "conventional oven 220°C for 20 to 25 minutes, fan oven 200°C". In the air fryer, set 190 to 200°C and start checking at 12 minutes, turning them at 6. Most batches finish between 12 and 16 minutes.

Packs that already give air fryer instructions are worth following, since the manufacturer has tested that product. If the pack says "not suitable for air fryer" or gives oven instructions only for a raw, stuffed chicken product, follow the pack.

Do You Thaw or Preheat for Frozen Food?

Do not thaw. Breaded, battered and pastry foods are designed to go from freezer to heat. Thawing lets the coating absorb moisture, so it turns soggy and can slide off in the basket. The exceptions are large items the chart does not cover, such as a whole frozen joint, which should be thawed in the fridge and cooked in the oven.

Do preheat, for 3 to 5 minutes. A hot basket starts setting the coating immediately, before the surface thaws and softens. If you skip preheating, add 2 to 3 minutes and expect slightly paler results.

Is It Pre-Cooked or Raw? Reading the Pack

Check this on every frozen meat product before you set a time. Two items that look identical in the freezer can need very different handling.

  • Pre-cooked ("fully cooked", "heat and serve", "ready to eat once heated"): most nuggets, chicken strips, meatballs, corn dogs and spring rolls. You are reheating them until hot through, so timing is about texture.
  • Raw ("uncooked", "raw", "cook thoroughly", "cook until 74°C/165°F"): some breaded chicken, many burgers, kofta, kibbeh and wings. These must reach a safe internal temperature, checked with a probe thermometer in the thickest part: 74°C (165°F) for chicken, 71°C (160°F) for minced beef or lamb.

One product needs special care. The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service says raw, stuffed, breaded chicken breast products (cheese-filled or Kiev-style) should not be cooked in an air fryer, and that you should follow the manufacturer's cooking instructions. They are browned on the outside but raw inside, so they look done long before they are. Use the oven method on the pack and confirm 74°C (165°F) in both the chicken and the filling.

How to Get Frozen Food Crisp, Not Soggy

Soggy frozen food is almost always a water problem. Ice on the surface melts and has to evaporate before the coating can crisp, and a crowded basket traps that steam.

  • Shake off loose ice before cooking. Frost from a half-used bag adds a surprising amount of water.
  • Leave space. A single layer is best. For fries and nuggets, fill the basket no more than halfway and shake every 5 to 6 minutes.
  • Add oil only where the pack does not. Par-fried foods such as fries, nuggets and mini spring rolls already contain oil. Unfried pastry, kibbeh and large spring rolls brown far better with a light mist of oil. Parathas already contain plenty of fat and need none.
  • Pour off water. Wings and burgers from frozen release liquid into the drawer. Tip it out halfway so it does not steam the food.
  • Rest briefly. Two minutes on a rack, not a plate, lets steam escape from the base.

Do not chase colour too far with potato products. The UK Food Standards Agency advises cooking starchy foods such as chips to a golden yellow colour or lighter, to limit acrylamide.

Iftar Batches: Samosas and Spring Rolls for a Crowd

During Ramadan, many Gulf households air fry two or three trays of frozen samosas, spring rolls and sambousek every evening. A 5 to 6 litre basket holds about 10 to 12 medium samosas in a single layer, so plan batches rather than piling them in.

  • Start the first batch 20 minutes before maghrib. Keep finished batches warm, uncovered, on a rack in a 100°C (210°F) oven so they stay crisp.
  • Cook the unfried pastry first. Thick-pastry samosas take the longest; pre-fried mini spring rolls only need 8 to 10 minutes and can go in last.
  • Homemade samosas freeze well. Freeze filled, unbaked samosas on a tray, then bag them. Brush with oil and cook from frozen at 180°C (350°F) for 14 to 18 minutes, turning once. A cooked filling just needs to be hot through; a raw minced meat filling must reach 71°C (160°F).
  • Reheating leftovers the next day takes 4 to 5 minutes at 170°C (340°F).

Troubleshooting Frozen Food in the Air Fryer

Problem Likely cause Fix
Soggy fries or nuggets Basket overfilled; ice on the surface Halve the batch, shake off ice and shake the basket more often
Outside burnt, middle still frozen Temperature too high for the thickness Drop 20°C and add time, or cover with foil for the first half
Pastry pale and dry Unfried pastry with no fat on the surface Mist with oil before cooking and again after turning
Cheese leaking out Overcooked, or thawed before cooking Cook from hard-frozen and stop at the first sign of oozing
Light items flying around Fan lifting thin waffles or wraps Place a heatproof trivet on top, or cook beside heavier items
White smoke Fat from wings or burgers burning in the drawer Pour off fat halfway and keep the drawer clean
Filling cold in the middle Thick item such as a burrito or lasagna Lower to 170–180°C, cover with foil and extend; check the centre

Smoke that keeps coming back usually means old grease has built up under the basket. Our guide to cleaning an air fryer covers the drawer and element.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you put frozen food straight into an air fryer?

Yes. Most frozen food, including fries, nuggets, fish fingers, samosas and spring rolls, should go straight from the freezer into a preheated air fryer without thawing. Thawing makes coatings soggy. Shake off loose ice, cook in a single layer and turn or shake the food halfway through.

Do you need to preheat the air fryer for frozen food?

Preheating for 3 to 5 minutes is worth it for frozen food. A hot basket sets breadcrumbs and pastry before they thaw and soften, so the result is crisper. If you skip it, add 2 to 3 minutes to the cooking time and check that the food is hot all the way through.

How do you convert oven times to air fryer times for frozen food?

Lower the conventional oven temperature on the pack by 15 to 20°C (25 to 35°F) and cut the time by 20 to 30%. If the pack gives a fan oven temperature, use that or 10°C less. Check at the halfway point and turn the food, then judge by colour and internal temperature.

Do you need to add oil to frozen food in an air fryer?

Usually not. Fries, nuggets, mini spring rolls and other par-fried foods already contain oil. Unfried items such as raw pastry samosas, kibbeh and large spring rolls brown much better with a light mist of oil. Parathas contain plenty of fat and need none.

Can you cook frozen lasagna in an air fryer?

Yes, if it is a single portion of about 300 to 400g. Move it to a heatproof dish that fits the basket, cover with foil and cook at 180°C (350°F) for 20 to 25 minutes, then uncover for 5 to 8 minutes. The centre should reach 74°C (165°F). Family-size trays are better in the oven.

How long do frozen waffles take in the air fryer?

Frozen toaster-style waffles take 4 to 6 minutes at 180°C (350°F) in a single layer. Flip them halfway if you want both sides equally crisp. They are done when the edges are crisp and the centre is hot and soft. Thicker Belgian-style waffles may need another minute.

Is it safe to cook frozen breaded chicken in an air fryer?

Pre-cooked breaded chicken such as nuggets and strips is safe to air fry; it only needs reheating. Raw breaded chicken must reach 74°C (165°F) in the centre. The USDA says raw, stuffed, breaded chicken breasts should not go in the air fryer, so use the oven method on the pack.

How long do frozen samosas take in the air fryer?

Frozen samosas with thick pastry take 12 to 15 minutes at 180°C (350°F), turned halfway. Thin-pastry samosas take 8 to 12 minutes at 190°C (375°F). Mist both kinds with a little oil if the pastry looks dry, and cook them from frozen in a single layer.
