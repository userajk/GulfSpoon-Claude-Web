28:T62f,Halwa puri is Pakistan's answer to the full English breakfast: a multi-dish spread that takes time, fills the house with noise and aroma, and turns a lazy morning into an event. The tradition is strongest in Punjab, particularly in Lahore, where halwa puri shops line every neighborhood and the Sunday morning queue starts before sunrise. But it belongs to all of Pakistan, with regional variations from Karachi to Peshawar, and it has traveled wherever Pakistanis have settled.

The breakfast is ancient in its components. Puri, unleavened deep-fried bread, has roots in the Indian subcontinent going back at least a thousand years. Sooji ka halwa, the sweet semolina dish, is descended from Arabic and Persian confections that arrived with the Mughal Empire. And channay, the spiced chickpea curry, draws on a tradition of legume cooking that has sustained South Asian populations for centuries. Somewhere along the way, these three elements merged into a single meal, and that meal became inseparable from the rhythm of Pakistani weekend life.

In the Gulf, halwa puri has become one of the most recognizable Pakistani food traditions. With roughly three million Pakistanis living across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, and Bahrain, the Friday morning breakfast has been recreated in apartments from Sharjah to Jeddah. 31:T419,Raw brats take 12 to 15 minutes at 190°C (375°F), turned once halfway. Thickness decides where you land in that range: a 3cm link is usually done at 13 minutes, a fat 3.5cm butcher's brat closer to 15. Chicken bratwurst cooks in about the same time as beef but must reach 74°C (165°F) rather than 71°C (160°F), so check it with a thermometer rather than going by colour.

At 200°C (400°F) raw brats are ready in 10 to 12 minutes, which is why many recipes use it. On thicker links, though, the casing tightens faster than the filling expands and often splits along one side, and juice runs out through the split. At 190°C that rarely happens.

Leave space between them. Brats that touch stay pale where they meet, because the hot air can't reach that strip of casing.

Oven-style air fryers with racks brown more slowly on the underside, so turn the brats twice instead of once and add 2 to 3 minutes. For more on why spacing and fan speed matter, see [how an air fryer actually cooks food](/beyond-the-recipes/how-does-an-air-fryer-work/).32:T568,**From frozen:** don't thaw them. If the links are frozen together, run the pack under cold water for 30 seconds and prise them apart with a butter knife. Always confirm the centre temperature, because frozen brats vary more than fresh ones.

**Precooked or smoked brats** are already safe to eat, so you're only reheating and browning them: 6 to 8 minutes at 190°C (375°F) until they're steaming hot in the middle.

**Simmered first:** if you like a very juicy brat with a softer bite, simmer raw brats for 10 minutes in salted water or chicken stock with a sliced onion, keeping it just below a boil so the casings don't burst.
