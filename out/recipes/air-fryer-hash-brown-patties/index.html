Air Fryer Hash Brown Patties (Frozen or Homemade)

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: November 20, 2026
Prep 2 mins
Cook 24 mins
Total 26 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Frozen hash brown patties go straight from the freezer into the air fryer and come out crisp on both faces in about 12 minutes, with no thawing and no frying pan. A homemade version from parboiled potatoes is included for when you want to make your own and freeze a batch.

Quick answer

Cook frozen hash brown patties at 200°C (400°F) for 10 to 12 minutes, flipping once at 6 minutes. Don't thaw them, and keep them in a single layer without touching. A light spray of oil on each side deepens the colour. They are done when both faces are deep golden and the edges feel firm and crisp.

Frozen hash brown patties are par-fried and shaped before they're frozen, so the air fryer only has to drive off the frost, re-crisp the oil already in the patty and heat the centre. In about 12 minutes you get something much closer to a deep-fried hash brown than an oven tray manages, and only the basket to wash.

Spacing is what most people get wrong. A patty that overlaps its neighbour steams where they touch and stays pale and soft in that spot. Lay them flat with a finger's width between each one, flip once halfway, and you get even colour on both faces.

If you would rather start from fresh potatoes, the homemade method below uses parboiled, grated potato so the patties hold together without egg, and they freeze well for quick weekday breakfasts. For a potato side with dinner rather than breakfast, our halved baby potatoes and roasted cubes and seasoned potato wedges use the same basket and a similar temperature.

How long to cook frozen hash brown patties in the air fryer

Standard frozen hash brown patties take 10 to 12 minutes at 200°C (400°F), flipped once at 6 minutes. Thin or mini patties are done in 8 to 10 minutes. Thick patties of 1.5cm or more do better at 190°C (375°F) for 13 to 16 minutes, so the centre heats through before the faces go too dark.

The number of patties barely changes the time, as long as they sit in one layer. What changes it is crowding: overlap two patties and the covered section needs several extra minutes and still won't crisp.

Package instructions are written for an oven or a frying pan, so treat them as a starting point only. The air fryer usually needs less time than the oven figure. For other frozen breakfast staples, our frozen food air fryer cooking times chart covers how to convert oven instructions.

Do you need to flip, thaw or oil hash brown patties?

Flip: yes. The underside sits on the basket and gets less airflow, so it stays paler. One flip at the halfway point gives both faces the same colour. A few air fryers with strong top-and-bottom airflow manage without it, but flipping costs ten seconds and removes the guesswork.

Thaw: no. Frozen patties hold their shape and go into a hot basket firm. Thawed ones soften, stick and tear when flipped. If yours have thawed by accident, cook them for 7 to 9 minutes and flip gently.

Oil: optional. Frozen patties are par-fried before freezing, so there is already oil in them. A light spray on each side gives a deeper colour and a slightly crunchier surface. Use a refillable pump sprayer or a brush rather than an aerosol can: the propellant in aerosols can leave a sticky residue that builds up on non-stick baskets.

How to make hash brown patties crispier

Soft patties almost always trace back to one of these:

  1. Preheat the basket. A cold start means the frost melts slowly and the patty sits in water.
  2. Leave gaps. At least 1cm between patties so air can reach the edges.
  3. Flip at halfway, not earlier. The first side needs time to set a crust that holds the patty together.
  4. Add 2 minutes at the end. If they are cooked but pale, an extra 2 minutes at 200°C (400°F) is usually all they need.
  5. Rest on a rack. Steam trapped under a patty on a plate softens the crust fast.

After flipping, press each patty lightly with a spatula. More of the surface touches the hot basket and the edges brown further.

How to make hash brown patties from scratch

Raw grated potato releases a lot of water and tends to fall apart in hot air. Parboil the potatoes whole first and the partly cooked starch binds the shreds together, which also come out drier.

  1. Put 600g whole, unpeeled floury potatoes in a pan of cold salted water. Bring to the boil and simmer for 8 minutes, until a knife goes in 1cm and then meets firm resistance. Do not cook them through.
  2. Drain and cool completely, at least 1 hour at room temperature or overnight in the fridge. Cold potato grates into clean shreds; warm potato turns to mash.
  3. Rub off the skins with your thumbs. Grate the potatoes on the coarse side of a box grater.
  4. Squeeze the grated onion hard in a tea towel, then mix it gently into the potato with the cornflour, salt and pepper. Use your fingertips so the shreds stay separate.
  5. Shape into 8 ovals about 1cm thick and 9cm long. Press them firmly so they hold together.
  6. Brush both sides with oil. Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 15 to 18 minutes, flipping at 9 minutes, until deep golden.

To freeze, lay the shaped, unbaked patties on a lined tray, freeze until solid, then stack them between squares of baking paper in a bag. Cook from frozen at 200°C (400°F) for 18 to 22 minutes, flipping at 10.

Are frozen hash brown patties halal?

Most frozen hash brown patties contain potato, vegetable oil, salt and a starch or dextrose coating, with no animal ingredients. Some brands add flavourings, and restaurant-style patties have in the past been made with beef flavouring or fried in animal fat. Read the ingredient list for "natural flavouring", "animal fat" or "beef tallow", and look for a halal certification mark, especially on imported brands. If the label is unclear, the homemade patties above contain nothing but potato, onion, cornflour, salt and oil.

For a full halal breakfast plate, serve the patties with crisp air fryer turkey bacon and jammy air fryer boiled eggs, or alongside a pan of Gulf-style shakshuka.

Air Fryer Hash Brown Patties

Scale:

Ingredients

Frozen patties

  • 8 frozen hash brown patties (standard oval patties, about 1cm (⅜ in) thick)
  • neutral oil spray (optional, sunflower or avocado oil)
  • 1 pinch flaky salt (to finish)

Homemade patties (makes 8)

  • 600 g floury potatoes (russet, Maris Piper or similar, about 3 medium (1⅓ lb), unpeeled)
  • ½ small onion (coarsely grated and squeezed dry)
  • 1 tbsp cornflour (cornstarch)
  • ¾ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (for brushing)

Instructions

  1. 1

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F) for 3 minutes. Patties that go into a hot basket start crisping immediately instead of sitting in thawing water.

  2. 2

    Take the patties straight from the freezer. Don't thaw them: thawed patties go limp and can break when you flip them.

  3. 3

    Lay the patties flat in a single layer with a small gap between each. Most 5 to 6 litre baskets fit 4 standard patties. Cook the rest in a second batch rather than overlapping them.

    If you want a deeper golden colour, give each patty a 1-second spray of oil on the top side now.

  4. 4

    Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 6 minutes. The top should be pale gold and the patty should hold its shape when nudged.

  5. 5

    Flip each patty with tongs or a thin spatula. Spray the new top side lightly with oil if you are using it.

  6. 6

    Cook for another 4 to 6 minutes, until both faces are deep golden and the edges feel firm and crisp when tapped.

  7. 7

    Sprinkle with flaky salt while they are still hot so it sticks, then serve straight away. Keep the first batch warm on a wire rack in a low oven, not on a plate, while the second batch cooks.

    A rack lets steam escape underneath. On a flat plate the bottom softens within a couple of minutes.

Equipment

Air fryer (5 to 6 litre basket) · Tongs or thin spatula · Wire rack · Box grater (homemade version)

Tips

  • Salt the patties the moment they come out. The surface is still slightly oily and the salt sticks.
  • Use tongs with silicone tips or a thin fish slice to flip. Metal tongs can scratch a non-stick basket and break the patty.
  • In an oven-style air fryer, use the middle shelf and swap trays halfway if you cook two.

Troubleshooting

  • Pale, soft patches: patties overlapped or touched. Leave a gap and cook in batches.
  • Patties broke when flipped: they were thawed, or flipped too early. Cook from frozen and wait until 6 minutes.
  • Dark outside, cold centre: the patties are thick or the air fryer runs hot. Drop to 190°C (375°F) and add 3 minutes.
  • Homemade patties fell apart: the potatoes were grated warm or over-boiled. Parboil for just 8 minutes and chill before grating.
  • Stuck to the basket: the basket was not preheated, or homemade patties were not oiled. Preheat and brush both sides.

Substitutions

  • No cornflour for the homemade version? Use 1 tbsp plain flour or potato starch, or 1 beaten egg if you don't need them egg-free.
  • Use ghee instead of oil when brushing homemade patties for a richer flavour.
  • Replace the onion with 2 finely sliced spring onions.

Variations

  • Cheesy: for the last 2 minutes, top each patty with a slice of cheddar or a spoonful of grated mozzarella.
  • Za'atar: sprinkle frozen patties with za'atar and a few drops of olive oil after flipping.
  • Spiced: mix ½ tsp each of cumin and chilli flakes and a handful of chopped coriander into the homemade mixture.
  • Breakfast stack: layer a patty, a fried egg, sliced tomato and halal turkey bacon in a toasted muffin.

What to Serve With It

  • With fried or scrambled eggs, grilled tomatoes and baked beans for a full breakfast.
  • Topped with smashed avocado, chilli flakes and a soft-boiled egg.
  • With labneh and a drizzle of chilli oil.
  • As a side for halal beef burgers instead of fries.

Storage

Cooked patties keep in the fridge for up to 3 days in an airtight container lined with paper towel. Cooked patties can also be frozen for up to 1 month, though uncooked homemade patties freeze better.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 190°C (375°F) for 3 to 4 minutes until the edges crisp again. Avoid the microwave, which makes them soft and chewy.

Make Ahead

Make the homemade patties up to 2 days ahead and keep them covered in the fridge on a lined tray, or freeze them uncooked for up to 2 months. Cook straight from the fridge for 15 to 18 minutes or from frozen for 18 to 22 minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you cook frozen hash brown patties in the air fryer?

Cook standard frozen hash brown patties at 200°C (400°F) for 10 to 12 minutes, flipping once at 6 minutes. Thin patties need 8 to 10 minutes and thick ones 13 to 16 minutes at 190°C (375°F). Don't thaw them first.

What temperature do you air fry hash brown patties at?

Use 200°C (400°F) for standard frozen patties and homemade patties. For extra-thick patties, use 190°C (375°F) so the inside heats through before the outside darkens too much.

Do you need to flip hash brown patties in the air fryer?

Yes, once at the halfway point. The side against the basket gets less airflow and stays paler. Flipping at about 6 minutes gives both faces an even golden crust.

Do you need to spray hash brown patties with oil in the air fryer?

No. Frozen patties are par-fried and already contain oil, so they crisp without extra. A light spray on each side gives a deeper golden colour and a slightly crunchier surface if you want it.

Can you cook hash brown patties in an air fryer oven?

Yes. Place them on the middle rack in a single layer and cook at 200°C (400°F) for 12 to 14 minutes, flipping at 7 minutes. If you use two trays, swap their positions when you flip.

Can you put frozen hash browns straight in the air fryer?

Yes, and it works better than thawing. Frozen patties stay firm enough to flip in one piece, while thawed ones go limp and tend to break. Cook at 200°C (400°F) for 10 to 12 minutes.

Why are my air fryer hash browns soggy?

Usually because the patties were touching, the basket was not preheated, or they were left on a plate after cooking. Leave gaps between them, preheat for 3 minutes and rest them on a wire rack.
