Air Fryer Salmon Bites with Honey Garlic Sauce

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: November 14, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 10 mins
Total 20 mins
Servings 3
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Cubes of skinless salmon dusted with cornflour and smoked paprika, air fried at high heat until the corners brown, then tossed in a quick honey garlic sauce. Dinner takes about 20 minutes, and the cube-size chart below tells you exactly when to pull them.

Quick answer

Air fry 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes of skinless salmon at 200°C (400°F) for 6 to 8 minutes, shaking the basket halfway, until the edges are browned and the largest cube flakes in the centre and reads 63°C (145°F). Toss the cubes in oil and a little cornflour before cooking, and add the honey garlic sauce afterwards.

Salmon bites are a fillet cut into 2.5cm cubes. Each cube has six faces in the hot air instead of a fillet's two, so every piece gets browned corners and the batch is done in about 7 minutes. Small pieces also overcook fast, which is why this recipe is timed by cube size rather than weight.

Cut the cubes evenly so they finish together. A teaspoon of cornflour tossed on with the oil and spices dries the surface and gives a thin, lightly crisp crust without breadcrumbs. The honey stays out of the basket: you simmer the sauce in a small pan for two minutes and toss the cooked bites through it, so nothing burns onto the tray.

If you want whole pieces of fish with crisp skin, our air fryer salmon fillets recipe is the one to follow. If your salmon is still a solid block in the freezer, use the timings in our frozen salmon method instead of cutting it into cubes.

How long do you air fry salmon bites?

Cook 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes for 6 to 8 minutes at 200°C (400°F). Cube size matters more than the total weight in the basket, because heat only has to travel about 1.25cm to reach the centre of each piece. Drop to 2cm cubes and you are done in 5 to 6 minutes. Go up to 4cm and you need closer to 10.

Keep the heat high. Browning (the Maillard reaction) only gets going once the surface is dry and well above 100°C, and at 180°C (350°F) the inside is cooked before the outside has time to colour.

Check the biggest cube, not the smallest. A probe thermometer pushed into the middle should read 63°C (145°F), the USDA safe temperature for fish. White beads on the surface are albumin, a protein squeezed out as the muscle fibres tighten. A few are normal; a crust of them means the salmon has gone a minute or two too far.

Oven-style air fryers with trays run slower, so rotate the tray halfway and expect to add a minute.

Why use cornflour, and why add the sauce after cooking?

Salmon is wet, and wet surfaces steam instead of browning. A teaspoon of cornflour tossed over oiled cubes soaks up that surface moisture and sets into a very thin crust in the hot air. You get crisp corners without a breadcrumb coating, and the sauce has something to cling to afterwards.

The honey goes in last because sugar scorches. At 200°C a glaze brushed on at the start blackens before the fish is cooked, and whatever drips through the basket smokes on the drip tray. Simmering the sauce in a pan for two minutes thickens it properly, and tossing the cooked bites through it coats every side. If you want stickier, slightly caramelised edges, put the glazed bites back on a parchment-lined basket for 1 minute at 200°C.

Honey garlic, spicy and other sauces for salmon bites

Each of these makes enough for 600g of salmon. Simmer the cooked sauces for 1 to 2 minutes; the dips need no cooking.

  • Spicy honey (hot honey style): 3 tbsp honey, 1½ tbsp sriracha, 1 tsp lemon juice, pinch of salt. Simmer and toss.
  • Teriyaki-style, alcohol-free: 2 tbsp halal soy sauce, 2 tbsp honey, 1 tsp grated ginger, 1 grated garlic clove, 3 tbsp water and ½ tsp cornflour stirred into the cold liquid. Simmer until glossy. Traditional teriyaki is made with alcohol-based seasonings; this version gets its shine from the cornflour instead.
  • Bang bang dip: 3 tbsp mayonnaise, 1 tbsp sweet chilli sauce, 1 tsp sriracha, squeeze of lime. Serve on the side so the bites stay crisp.
  • Lemon and sumac yoghurt: 120g thick yoghurt or labneh, 1 tsp sumac, zest of half a lemon, pinch of salt. A cooling dip for summer, when a sticky glaze feels heavy.

Which salmon to buy for salmon bites

Buy a centre-cut fillet about 2.5 to 3cm thick. It gives you even cubes with little waste, whereas the tail end is thin enough to dry out before the thicker pieces are cooked. Skinless saves you a job, and most fish counters will remove the skin if you ask.

Farmed Atlantic salmon, the kind most Gulf supermarkets stock, has more fat between the flakes and stays juicy if you overshoot by a minute. Wild sockeye or coho is leaner and finishes about a minute sooner, so start checking at 5 minutes.

Frozen portions work if you thaw them first. Move them to the fridge the night before, or cut them while still half-frozen, when the flesh is firm and slices cleanly, then let the cubes finish thawing for 20 minutes and pat them dry before seasoning. Serve the bites over rice with air fryer asparagus or a tray of crispy roasted chickpeas for crunch.

Air Fryer Salmon Bites

Scale:

Ingredients

Salmon bites

  • 600 g skinless salmon fillet (1.3 lb, ideally a centre cut about 2.5cm (1 inch) thick)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil or avocado oil
  • 1 tsp cornflour (cornstarch)
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • ½ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Honey garlic sauce

  • 3 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (choose a halal-certified brand; some brewed soy sauces list alcohol as a preservative)
  • 2 cloves garlic (finely grated)
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger (grated, optional)
  • 2 tsp lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp water
  • ¼ tsp chilli flakes (optional)

To finish

  • 1 tsp sesame seeds
  • 2 spring onions (thinly sliced)

Instructions

  1. 1

    If the fillet has skin, lay it skin-side down, cut a small flap of flesh away from the skin at the thin end, grip the skin with a paper towel and run a sharp knife along it at a low angle while pulling the skin towards you. Pat the fillet dry on both sides.

    Salmon cuts more cleanly when it is cold. Cut it straight from the fridge, or give a soft fillet 15 minutes in the freezer.

  2. 2

    Cut the salmon into 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes. Fold thin tail and belly pieces over on themselves or cut them a little larger so every piece is roughly the same thickness.

  3. 3

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F) for 3 minutes. In a bowl, toss the cubes with the oil first, then sprinkle over the cornflour, garlic powder, smoked paprika, salt and pepper and toss again until no dry powder is visible.

  4. 4

    Lightly oil the basket and spread the cubes out in a single layer with about 1cm between them. Air fry for 4 minutes.

    In a 3 to 4 litre basket, cook the batch in two rounds. Crowded cubes steam each other and stay pale.

  5. 5

    Shake the basket or turn the cubes with tongs, then air fry for another 2 to 4 minutes. They are ready when the corners are browned, the flesh has turned opaque all the way through, a cube flakes when pressed, and the largest piece reads 63°C (145°F) on a probe thermometer.

  6. 6

    While the salmon cooks, put the honey, soy sauce, garlic, ginger, lemon juice, water and chilli flakes in a small pan over medium heat. Simmer for 1 to 2 minutes, stirring, until the bubbles turn large and glossy and the sauce coats the back of a spoon.

    Take it off the heat when it looks like runny honey. It thickens as it cools.

  7. 7

    Tip the hot salmon bites into a wide bowl, pour over the sauce and fold gently with a spatula until coated. Stirring hard breaks the cubes apart.

  8. 8

    Scatter with sesame seeds and spring onion and serve straight away, while the edges still have some bite.

Equipment

Air fryer · Sharp knife · Mixing bowl · Small saucepan · Tongs · Probe thermometer

Tips

  • Start checking at 6 minutes the first time you make these. Air fryers vary, and one extra minute takes salmon from juicy to dry.
  • Make the sauce while the salmon cooks. Warm sauce coats hot fish evenly; cold sauce clumps on it.

Troubleshooting

  • Bites are pale and soft: the basket was crowded or the salmon was wet. Cook in a single layer with gaps, pat the cubes dry, and make sure the air fryer was preheated.
  • Covered in white beads and dry: overcooked. Pull them at 63°C (145°F) and check a minute earlier next time.
  • Some cubes are raw while others are dry: the pieces were different sizes. Fold thin tail pieces or cut them larger.
  • Sauce burned in the basket: the glaze went on too early. Toss cooked bites in the sauce in a bowl instead.

Substitutions

  • No cornflour? Use the same amount of potato starch or rice flour.
  • Swap honey for date syrup (dibs) in the sauce. It is less runny, so add an extra tablespoon of water.
  • Coconut aminos replace soy sauce for a soy-free or gluten-free version; reduce the honey by 1 tbsp because aminos are sweeter.
  • Trout or firm white fish such as hammour or kingfish work with the same method. White fish is leaner, so check a minute early.

Variations

  • Panko-crusted: toss the oiled cubes in 40g panko mixed with the spices, spray with oil and air fry 8 to 9 minutes.
  • Za'atar and lemon: skip the paprika, coat with 1½ tsp za'atar, and finish with lemon zest and a drizzle of olive oil instead of the honey sauce.
  • Cajun-spiced: replace the paprika and garlic with 1½ tsp Cajun seasoning and serve with the bang bang dip.

What to Serve With It

  • Make a salmon bites bowl: rice, sliced cucumber, avocado, shredded carrot, spring onion and the bang bang dip.
  • Serve with plain basmati rice and a lemony green salad for a weeknight dinner.
  • Pile into warm flatbread with the sumac yoghurt, shredded lettuce and pickled turnips.
  • Put them out with cocktail sticks and two dips as a starter for a family iftar.

Storage

Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container within 2 hours of cooking and eat within 2 days. Store sauced and plain bites separately if you can, because the glaze softens the crust overnight.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 160°C (320°F) for 3 to 4 minutes, until hot in the centre. A lower temperature than the original cook warms the fish through without drying it. Avoid the microwave, which makes salmon rubbery and fishy-smelling.

Make Ahead

Cube and season the salmon up to 4 hours ahead and keep it covered in the fridge, but add the cornflour just before cooking so it does not turn pasty. The honey garlic sauce keeps for 5 days in the fridge; warm it for 20 seconds to loosen.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you cook salmon bites in the air fryer?

Cook 2.5cm (1 inch) salmon cubes for 6 to 8 minutes at 200°C (400°F), shaking halfway. Smaller 2cm cubes take 5 to 6 minutes and larger 4cm cubes take 8 to 10. They are done when the centre flakes and reads 63°C (145°F).

Should I remove the skin before making salmon bites?

Yes, for the best texture. Skin on small cubes does not crisp well because it cannot lie flat against the hot air, so it turns chewy. Remove it before cutting, or ask the fish counter to skin the fillet for you.

Can I make salmon bites with frozen salmon?

Yes, but thaw it first. Cubes cut from fully frozen fish release water as they cook, which stops the seasoning sticking and prevents browning. Thaw fillets overnight in the fridge, or cut them while half-frozen and let the cubes finish thawing for 20 minutes, then pat dry.

How do you keep salmon bites from sticking to the air fryer basket?

Lightly oil the basket, toss the cubes in oil before the dry seasoning, and wait 4 minutes before shaking. Once a crust forms the cubes release cleanly. A perforated parchment liner also works, but it slightly reduces browning underneath.

Are air fryer salmon bites healthy?

They are a good source of protein and omega-3 fats, and the air fryer needs only a tablespoon of oil for 600g of fish. The NHS recommends at least one portion of oily fish such as salmon a week. The honey sauce adds sugar, so use less or serve it on the side.
