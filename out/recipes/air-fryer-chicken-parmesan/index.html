Air Fryer Chicken Parmesan With a Crust That Stays Crisp

By Omar Hussain|Updated: November 16, 2026
Prep 25 mins
Cook 26 mins
Total 51 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Medium
Cuisine Italian-American

Panko and Parmesan crusted chicken cutlets, air fried until crisp, then topped with a thick homemade marinara and mozzarella for the last 2 minutes. The sauce is a quick halal-friendly marinara, and the cheese stage is timed so the crust underneath stays crunchy.

Quick answer

Air fry breaded chicken parmesan cutlets at 200°C (400°F) for 8 minutes, flip, then cook 3 to 5 minutes more until the centre reads 74°C (165°F). Spoon thick marinara onto the middle, add mozzarella and air fry 2 to 3 minutes until the cheese bubbles. Pound the chicken to an even 1.5 cm first.

Chicken parmesan asks for a crunchy crust with wet sauce and melted cheese sitting on top of it. The air fryer gets the crust right because the fan dries and browns the panko from every side at once. Keeping it crunchy under the topping comes down to timing: sauce and cheese go on only after the chicken is fully cooked, and they get 2 to 3 minutes, enough to melt mozzarella before the tomato has time to soak in.

The method is the same three-step dredge used for our crumbed chicken tenders, with two changes. The panko is mixed with grated hard cheese and rubbed with a tablespoon of olive oil before it touches the chicken, so it browns evenly without patchy spraying. And the chicken is pounded to an even 1.5 cm, so the thin edges don't dry out while the thick end catches up.

The marinara is a 15-minute pan sauce of tinned tomatoes, garlic, oregano and olive oil. It contains no alcohol, and it's cooked thicker than most jarred sauces, which matters here. If you'd rather skip the crumb coating, the section below covers a no-breading version built on plain seasoned thin cutlets.

How do you keep chicken parmesan crisp under the sauce?

Use a thick sauce, leave a bare border, and add it only for the final 2 to 3 minutes. Sogginess comes from water moving out of the tomato into the crumb. A sauce reduced until it holds a trail has far less free water than a runny jarred sauce, and the bare 1 to 2 cm border around each cutlet stays as crunchy as it was before the topping went on.

Cheese choice matters for the same reason. Low-moisture mozzarella (the firm block, not the balls in brine) melts into a stretchy layer without weeping. Fresh mozzarella releases liquid as it heats; if you want to use it, tear it, pat it dry on kitchen paper and add it for no more than 2 minutes.

Finally, rest the cooked cutlets on a rack rather than a flat plate. Steam escaping from the bottom of a hot cutlet condenses against a plate and softens the underside within a few minutes.

How long to cook chicken parmesan in the air fryer

For a 1.5 cm cutlet, about 13 to 16 minutes in total at 200°C (400°F): 8 minutes on the first side, 3 to 5 after flipping, then 2 to 3 minutes with the sauce and cheese. The chart above gives times for thinner and thicker cutlets and for crumbed cutlets cooked straight from the freezer.

Thickness drives the time, which is why pounding is worth the effort. A whole breast is often 3 cm at one end and 1 cm at the other, so by the time the centre reaches 74°C (165°F) the thin end is dry. Check the thickest point with a thermometer before the sauce goes on; once the cheese is on, it's hard to get a clean reading.

Which cheese to use, and a note on Parmesan and rennet

Traditional Parmigiano Reggiano is made with calf rennet, so many halal cooks avoid it unless the pack says otherwise. Parmesan-style hard cheeses made with microbial or vegetable rennet grate and brown the same way, and halal-certified grated cheeses are sold in most large Gulf supermarkets. Check the ingredients for "microbial rennet" or a halal mark. The same check applies to mozzarella and to any shop-bought breaded chicken.

For the topping, grate the mozzarella yourself if you can. Pre-grated bags are tossed in starch to stop clumping, and that starch slows melting, which means more minutes in the air fryer and more time for the crust to soften.

Chicken parm without breading

Skip the flour, egg and crumb, brush 1 cm cutlets with oil and season them with salt, pepper, oregano and garlic powder. Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 6 to 8 minutes, flipping halfway, until they read 74°C (165°F), then add sauce and cheese for 2 minutes. It's lighter and faster, but without a crust the sauce sits directly on the meat, so serve it straight away, ideally with garlic bread for the sauce. Cooked cutlets left over from meal prep work too: reheat them to 74°C (165°F) first, then top. Melted cheese that drips through the basket holes is easiest to remove while the basket is still warm; our air fryer cleaning guide covers soaking it off.

Air Fryer Chicken Parmesan

Scale:

Ingredients

Quick marinara

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic (finely chopped)
  • 400 g crushed tomatoes (1 tin (14 oz))
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • ¼ tsp sugar (only if the tomatoes taste sharp)
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 pinch chilli flakes (optional)

Chicken and crumb

  • 2 large boneless, skinless chicken breasts (about 500g (1.1 lb) total)
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 40 g plain flour (⅓ cup)
  • 2 eggs (beaten with 1 tbsp water)
  • 60 g panko breadcrumbs (1¼ cups)
  • 30 g finely grated Parmesan-style hard cheese (⅓ cup; microbial rennet or halal-certified)
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (plus a little for the basket)

Topping

  • 150 g low-moisture mozzarella (grated or thinly sliced (5 oz))
  • 2 tbsp finely grated Parmesan-style hard cheese
  • 1 handful fresh basil leaves (to finish)

Instructions

  1. 1

    Make the marinara. Warm the olive oil in a small pan over medium heat, add the garlic and stir for about 1 minute until it smells sweet but hasn't browned. Add the tomatoes, oregano, salt, sugar and chilli flakes, then simmer uncovered for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until a spoon dragged across the base leaves a clear trail.

    A thick sauce carries less water into the crust. If yours is still loose, give it 5 more minutes rather than spooning on extra.

  2. 2

    Slice each chicken breast in half horizontally to make 4 cutlets. Lay them between two sheets of baking paper and pound the thick end with a rolling pin or the base of a pan until each cutlet is an even 1.5 cm (⅝ in) thick. Season both sides with the salt and pepper.

  3. 3

    Set out three shallow dishes: flour in the first, beaten egg in the second, and in the third the panko, 30g grated cheese, oregano and garlic powder. Drizzle the 1 tbsp olive oil over the panko mix and rub it between your fingers until every crumb looks slightly damp.

  4. 4

    Coat each cutlet in flour and shake off the excess, dip it in egg and let the drips fall away, then press it firmly into the crumb on both sides. Rest the coated cutlets on a rack for 5 minutes while the air fryer heats.

    The rest lets the flour and egg set into a glue, so the coating doesn't slide off when you flip.

  5. 5

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F) for 3 to 5 minutes. Brush the basket lightly with oil and lay in 2 cutlets with a gap between them. A 5 to 6 litre basket fits two; a dual-basket or oven-style air fryer can take all four.

  6. 6

    Air fry for 8 minutes, flip, then cook for 3 to 5 minutes more. The crust should be deep golden and the thickest part should read 74°C (165°F) on an instant-read thermometer.

  7. 7

    Spoon 2 tablespoons of marinara into the centre of each cutlet and spread it out, leaving a 1 to 2 cm border of bare crust. Cover the sauce with mozzarella, press it down lightly and scatter over the 2 tbsp grated cheese. Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 2 to 3 minutes until the mozzarella has melted and blistered in spots.

    Pressing the cheese into the sauce stops the fan from blowing shreds off the chicken.

  8. 8

    Lift the cutlets onto a rack or plate, rest for 2 minutes so the cheese sets, and repeat with the second batch. Finish with basil and serve with the rest of the marinara, warmed.

Equipment

Air fryer (5 to 6 litre basket) · Small saucepan · Rolling pin or meat mallet · Baking paper · Three shallow dishes · Instant-read thermometer · Wire rack

Tips

  • Rub the oil into the panko instead of spraying the cutlets. Spray tends to land in patches, which gives you pale and dark spots on the same piece.
  • Season the chicken itself, not just the crumb. Salt on the meat under the coating is what makes the centre taste of something.
  • Don't add the sauce before the chicken reaches 74°C (165°F). Once the cheese is on, any extra cooking softens the crust.
  • If the coating darkens before the centre is cooked, your cutlets are too thick or your air fryer runs hot. Drop to 190°C (375°F) for the next batch.
  • Keep the first batch crisp on a rack in a 90°C (200°F) oven while the second batch cooks.

Troubleshooting

  • Coating fell off when flipping: usually a skipped flour layer, or cutlets that went into the crumb dripping with egg. Shake off the excess at each stage and give the coated chicken its 5-minute rest.
  • Crust soggy under the cheese: reduce the sauce until it holds a trail, leave a bare border, and keep it to the last 2 to 3 minutes.
  • Cheese blew around the basket: press it into the sauce, or use thin slices instead of shreds.
  • Outside dark, middle under 74°C (165°F): the cutlet is too thick for 200°C. Pound it thinner next time, or cook at 190°C (375°F) for longer.

Substitutions

  • Chicken thighs: use boneless, skinless thighs, flattened to 1.5 cm. They take 1 to 2 minutes longer and stay juicier if slightly overcooked.
  • Gluten-free: use rice flour for the first dredge and gluten-free panko or crushed cornflakes for the crumb.
  • Egg-free: whisk 3 tbsp thick yoghurt with 1 tbsp water and use it in place of the egg. (Regular mayonnaise is made with egg, so only egg-free mayonnaise works here.)
  • Jarred sauce: use a thick tomato and basil pasta sauce and read the label. Choose one with a halal mark and a plain tomato, herb and oil ingredient list.

Variations

  • Aubergine parmesan: slice a large aubergine into 1.5 cm rounds, salt for 20 minutes, pat dry and bread as above. Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 10 to 12 minutes, flipping halfway, then top and melt.
  • Parm sandwich: slice a cooked cutlet and put it in a toasted roll with extra sauce and a few basil leaves.
  • Za'atar crumb: swap the oregano in the crumb for 2 tsp za'atar.

What to Serve With It

  • Over spaghetti or penne tossed with the leftover marinara.
  • With a rocket and tomato salad dressed with lemon and olive oil.
  • Alongside air fryer garlic bread for mopping up the sauce.

Storage

Store cooked, topped cutlets in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Keep extra marinara separately in a jar for up to 5 days.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 180°C (350°F) for 4 to 6 minutes until hot through (74°C / 165°F in the centre). The crust recovers most of its crunch; a microwave leaves it soft.

Make Ahead

Bread the cutlets, freeze them on a tray until solid, then bag them for up to 2 months. Cook from frozen at 190°C (375°F) for about 10 minutes, flip, cook 6 to 8 minutes more until 74°C (165°F), then add sauce and cheese for 3 minutes. The marinara can be made up to 5 days ahead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you make chicken parmesan in an air fryer?

Yes. The air fryer browns a panko crust on all sides at once, so you don't need to shallow fry. Cook the breaded chicken fully first at 200°C (400°F), then add sauce and mozzarella for the last 2 to 3 minutes so the crust stays crisp.

What temperature do you air fry chicken parm at?

Use 200°C (400°F) for cutlets up to 1.5 cm thick. That's hot enough to brown the crumb before the chicken dries out. For thicker pieces or frozen breaded cutlets, use 190°C (375°F) and cook longer so the crust doesn't burn before the centre reaches 74°C (165°F).

How long does chicken parmesan take in the air fryer?

About 13 to 16 minutes for a 1.5 cm cutlet: 8 minutes, a flip, 3 to 5 minutes more, then 2 to 3 minutes with the sauce and cheese. Thinner cutlets need less. Confirm 74°C (165°F) in the thickest part before adding the topping.

How do you reheat chicken parmesan in the air fryer?

Air fry it at 180°C (350°F) for 4 to 6 minutes, straight from the fridge, until the centre is hot. The lower temperature warms the chicken through before the cheese and crust overbrown. Let it sit for a minute before cutting.

How do you cook frozen chicken parmesan in the air fryer?

Cook frozen breaded cutlets at 190°C (375°F) for about 10 minutes, flip, and cook 6 to 8 minutes more until the centre reads 74°C (165°F). Then add sauce and cheese for 3 minutes. For shop-bought frozen chicken parmesan, follow the pack and check the temperature.

Does Parmesan melt in the air fryer?

Finely grated Parmesan-style cheese softens and browns rather than melting into strings. That's why it goes in the crumb and is sprinkled on top for colour, while mozzarella does the melting. In 2 to 3 minutes at 200°C (400°F) it turns golden in spots.

Can I make air fryer chicken parmesan without breading?

Yes. Brush 1 cm cutlets with oil, season them and air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 6 to 8 minutes, flipping halfway, until they reach 74°C (165°F). Add sauce and cheese for 2 minutes. Serve straight away, since there's no crust to protect the meat from the sauce.
