Crispy Air Fryer Marinated Tofu Cubes

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: September 24, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 15 mins
Total 45 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Crispy, golden tofu cubes with a shattering cornstarch crust and a tender, flavor-soaked interior — made in the air fryer with barely a tablespoon of oil. A quick soy-garlic-ginger marinade seasons the tofu all the way through, and a light cornstarch coating crisps it to deep-fried perfection.

Tofu in the air fryer is a revelation for anyone who has struggled with soggy, bland tofu on the stovetop. The circulating hot air crisps every surface of the cube evenly, producing a crunch that rivals deep frying — without a pot of oil or the mess that comes with it. The key is pressing the tofu first to remove excess water, then marinating it briefly so it absorbs flavor before the cornstarch coating goes on.

Most tofu recipes fail for one of two reasons: not enough seasoning or not enough texture. This recipe solves both. The soy-garlic-ginger marinade saturates the tofu with umami, and the cornstarch coating creates a thin, crispy shell that shatters when you bite into it. The contrast between the crunchy exterior and the soft, creamy interior is what makes this recipe work.

Pressing the tofu is non-negotiable. Tofu is packed with water, and that water prevents both seasoning absorption and crispiness. Wrapping it in paper towels and pressing it under a heavy object for 15 to 30 minutes removes enough moisture to transform the texture completely. If you cook tofu straight from the package without pressing, no amount of cornstarch will save it.

In the Gulf, tofu has found a growing audience among health-conscious eaters and the vegetarian and vegan communities across Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and the wider region. This recipe works as a protein for grain bowls, a stir-fry component, a salad topper, or simply as a snack with dipping sauce.

Pro Tip: Use extra-firm tofu. Firm works in a pinch, but silken or soft tofu will crumble and stick to the basket.

Air Fryer Marinated Tofu

Scale:

Ingredients

Tofu

  • 400 g extra-firm tofu (one block — pressed for 15 to 30 minutes)
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp rice vinegar
  • 2 cloves garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 2 tbsp cornstarch
  • cooking oil spray (light mist)

Optional Glaze

  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sriracha or chili garlic sauce
  • 1 tbsp honey or maple syrup
  • sesame seeds (for garnish)
  • green onions, sliced (for garnish)

Instructions

  1. 1

    Press the tofu: drain the block, wrap it in several layers of paper towels or a clean kitchen towel, and place a heavy object on top (a cast iron pan or a stack of plates). Press for 15 to 30 minutes.

    This step is non-negotiable. Unpressed tofu is too waterlogged to crisp or absorb marinade. Longer pressing gives better results.

  2. 2

    Cut the pressed tofu into bite-sized cubes — about 2cm (¾ inch) pieces.

  3. 3

    In a bowl, whisk together the soy sauce, sesame oil, rice vinegar, minced garlic, and grated ginger. Add the tofu cubes and toss gently to coat. Let marinate for 15 minutes, tossing once halfway through.

    Do not marinate for more than 30 minutes — the tofu can become too salty and start to break apart.

  4. 4

    Drain any excess marinade from the tofu. Sprinkle the cornstarch over the cubes and toss gently until every piece is evenly coated.

    The cornstarch is the crunch layer. Make sure every surface is covered — any bare spots will stay soft.

  5. 5

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F).

  6. 6

    Arrange the tofu cubes in a single layer in the air fryer basket. Leave space between each piece. Spray lightly with cooking oil.

  7. 7

    Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 12 to 15 minutes, shaking the basket or flipping the cubes at the halfway mark. Cook until golden brown and crispy on all sides.

  8. 8

    If using the glaze: whisk together soy sauce, sriracha, and honey. Toss the hot tofu in the glaze immediately after removing from the air fryer. Garnish with sesame seeds and sliced green onions. Serve immediately.

Tips

  • Use extra-firm tofu. Firm tofu works but is more delicate. Silken or soft tofu will crumble.
  • Press the tofu for at least 15 minutes. More pressing = better results. You can press for up to an hour.
  • Cornstarch creates the crispy coating. Without it, the tofu will be soft on the outside.
  • Do not overcrowd the basket. Tofu needs air on all sides to crisp evenly. Cook in batches if necessary.
  • Shake the basket or flip the cubes at the halfway mark for even browning on all sides.
  • Serve immediately. Tofu loses its crispiness faster than chicken — within 10 to 15 minutes, the cornstarch coating softens.

Substitutions

  • No cornstarch? Arrowroot powder or tapioca starch work the same way. Regular flour also works but produces a slightly less crispy result.
  • Replace soy sauce with tamari for a gluten-free version.
  • No sesame oil? Use any neutral oil. The sesame oil adds flavor but is not essential.
  • For a no-marinade shortcut, simply season pressed tofu cubes with salt, garlic powder, and a spray of oil before coating in cornstarch.

Storage

Store leftover tofu in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in the air fryer at 190°C (375°F) for 4 to 5 minutes to restore some crispness. Microwaving will make it soft.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I make crispy tofu in the air fryer?

Three essential steps: press the tofu for 15 to 30 minutes to remove water, coat it in cornstarch for a crispy shell, and cook at 200°C (400°F) for 12 to 15 minutes with space between each piece.

Do I need to press tofu before air frying?

Yes. Tofu is full of water that prevents both crispy texture and flavor absorption. Press it for at least 15 minutes — wrap in paper towels and place a heavy object on top.

What type of tofu is best for the air fryer?

Extra-firm tofu. It holds its shape, absorbs marinade well, and crisps the best. Firm tofu works but is more fragile. Avoid silken or soft tofu — they will crumble.

Can I air fry tofu without cornstarch?

You can, but the outside will not be crispy. The cornstarch creates a thin shell that crisps in the hot air. Without it, the tofu will be golden but soft-textured on the outside.

Why is my air fryer tofu still soggy?

Three likely causes: the tofu was not pressed long enough, the basket was overcrowded (causing steaming), or you skipped the cornstarch. All three contribute to sogginess.
