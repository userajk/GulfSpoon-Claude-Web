Air Fryer Brats with Peppers and Onions (Halal Beef or Chicken)

By Omar Hussain|Updated: November 12, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 18 mins
Total 28 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Halal beef or chicken bratwurst cooked in the air fryer on a bed of sliced peppers and onions, so the sausage fat seasons the vegetables while the casings brown. Dinner is on the table in about 25 minutes, with a chart for precooked and frozen brats.

Quick answer

Cook raw halal bratwurst in the air fryer at 190°C (375°F) for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once halfway, until the casings are deep brown and taut and the centre reads 71°C (160°F) for beef or 74°C (165°F) for chicken. Don't pierce them first. Precooked brats need 6 to 8 minutes.

Bratwurst needs very little attention in an air fryer. The fan browns the casing all the way round and the fat drips away instead of pooling, so there's no spitting pan to stand over. This version cooks halal beef or chicken bratwurst on top of sliced peppers and onions, so the vegetables catch the drippings and soften while the sausages brown.

Get the temperature right. Bratwurst are thick (usually 3 to 3.5cm across) and packed tightly, so at 200°C the casing can split before the centre is cooked. At 190°C (375°F) the outside browns steadily and the inside reaches a safe temperature at about the same moment.

If you want a starchy side cooked in the same machine, roast a batch of crispy cubed potatoes or sweet potato fries first and keep them warm in a low oven while the brats cook.

How long do you cook brats in the air fryer?

Raw brats take 12 to 15 minutes at 190°C (375°F), turned once halfway. Thickness decides where you land in that range: a 3cm link is usually done at 13 minutes, a fat 3.5cm butcher's brat closer to 15. Chicken bratwurst cooks in about the same time as beef but must reach 74°C (165°F) rather than 71°C (160°F), so check it with a thermometer rather than going by colour.

At 200°C (400°F) raw brats are ready in 10 to 12 minutes, which is why many recipes use it. On thicker links, though, the casing tightens faster than the filling expands and often splits along one side, and juice runs out through the split. At 190°C that rarely happens.

Leave space between them. Brats that touch stay pale where they meet, because the hot air can't reach that strip of casing.

Oven-style air fryers with racks brown more slowly on the underside, so turn the brats twice instead of once and add 2 to 3 minutes. For more on why spacing and fan speed matter, see how an air fryer actually cooks food.

Cooking brats from frozen, precooked or simmered

From frozen: don't thaw them. Start raw frozen brats at 180°C (350°F) for 10 minutes, so the centre thaws before the casing over-browns, then turn them, raise the heat to 200°C (400°F) and cook for another 8 to 10 minutes. If the links are frozen together, run the pack under cold water for 30 seconds and prise them apart with a butter knife. Always confirm the centre temperature, because frozen brats vary more than fresh ones.

Precooked or smoked brats are already safe to eat, so you're only reheating and browning them: 6 to 8 minutes at 190°C (375°F) until they're steaming hot in the middle.

Simmered first: if you like a very juicy brat with a softer bite, simmer raw brats for 10 minutes in salted water or chicken stock with a sliced onion, keeping it just below a boil so the casings don't burst. Drain, pat them dry, then air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 4 to 6 minutes to brown the outside. Simmering is optional; the straight air-fryer method cooks them through safely on its own.

If you'd rather skip the peppers, cook the brats over thick roasted onion wedges instead, using the same 5-minute head start. Beef brats leave a film of fat in the drawer either way; wipe it out while it's still warm, and see our air fryer cleaning guide for grease that has already baked on.

Finding halal bratwurst and checking the label

Traditional German bratwurst isn't halal, and almost every other recipe online assumes the traditional kind. This page is written for halal beef or chicken bratwurst only.

In the Gulf, halal beef and chicken sausages, including bratwurst-style links, are usually in the chilled sausage and cold-cuts section of larger supermarkets and hypermarkets, with frozen packs in the freezer aisle nearby. Where non-halal meat is sold at all, it sits in a separate, signposted non-Muslim section, so it's easy to avoid. Some halal butchers also make fresh beef sausages to order if you ask for a coarse grind with nutmeg, white pepper and marjoram, the classic bratwurst seasoning.

Outside the Gulf, look for a recognised halal certification mark on the pack, not just the word "beef". Check the casing line in the ingredients: collagen casings made from beef, or sheep casings, are what you want. A pack that says "natural casing" with no halal mark could have a non-halal casing even if the meat is beef.

Air Fryer Brats with Peppers and Onions

Scale:

Ingredients

Brats

  • 4 halal beef or chicken bratwurst (raw, about 100–120g (3½–4 oz) each, 450–500g (1 lb) in total)

Peppers and Onions

  • 1 red pepper (sliced into 1cm (½ inch) strips)
  • 1 green pepper (sliced into 1cm (½ inch) strips)
  • 1 large onion (halved and cut into 1cm (½ inch) slices)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil or sunflower oil
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • ¼ tsp ground cumin (optional)

To Serve

  • 4 hot dog rolls or brioche rolls (split)
  • mustard (yellow or wholegrain; choose a halal-certified, alcohol-free jar)
  • pickled chillies or gherkins (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1

    Take the bratwurst out of the fridge 10 minutes before cooking and pat them dry with paper towels. Leave the casings whole.

    Piercing lets the juices run out and gives you a drier sausage. A dry surface browns faster, which is why the pat-down matters.

  2. 2

    Toss the sliced peppers and onion with the oil, salt, smoked paprika and cumin in a bowl until every strip is lightly coated.

  3. 3

    Preheat the air fryer to 190°C (375°F) for 3 minutes.

  4. 4

    Spread the peppers and onions in the basket and air fry for 5 minutes. They need this head start because they take longer to soften than the brats take to cook.

  5. 5

    Stir the vegetables, then lay the brats on top in a single layer with 1 to 2cm between them. Air fry for 7 minutes.

    In a basket smaller than 4 litres, cook the vegetables and brats separately and combine them at the end.

  6. 6

    Turn each brat with tongs and stir the vegetables underneath. Air fry for another 5 to 8 minutes, until the casings are deep brown and tight and an instant-read thermometer in the thickest end reads 71°C (160°F) for beef or 74°C (165°F) for chicken.

  7. 7

    Move the brats and vegetables to a plate and let the brats rest for 3 minutes. Put the split rolls in the empty basket, cut side up, and toast at 190°C (375°F) for 1 to 2 minutes until the edges are crisp.

  8. 8

    Put a brat in each roll, pile the peppers and onions on top and finish with mustard and pickled chillies.

Equipment

Air fryer (5–6 litre basket) · Instant-read thermometer · Tongs · Mixing bowl

Tips

  • Use a thermometer the first time you cook a new brand. Halal brats range from lean chicken links to fatty beef ones, and fat content changes the timing by a few minutes.
  • Turn with tongs, not a fork. Every hole you poke lets juice escape.
  • If the casings look wrinkled and pale at the turn, the basket was too crowded or not preheated. Spread them out and add 2 minutes.
  • Cut the peppers and onion to the same 1cm thickness so they soften at the same rate.
  • Fat from beef brats can smoke slightly as it hits the drawer. If your manual allows it, a tablespoon of water in the drawer under the basket stops the smoke and makes cleaning easier.

Troubleshooting

  • Casings split: the heat was too high or the brats were very cold. Cook at 190°C (375°F), not 200°C, and let them sit out for 10 minutes first.
  • Brown outside, undercooked inside: the links are thicker than average. Lower to 180°C (350°F) and cook a few minutes longer, checking for 71°C (160°F) or 74°C (165°F).
  • Pale, soft casings: the basket was crowded or wet. Pat the brats dry and leave space between them.
  • Vegetables still crunchy: the strips were too thick or went in at the same time as the brats. Give them the 5-minute head start.
  • Dry, shrivelled brats: they were pierced or overcooked. Pull them as soon as they reach temperature.

Substitutions

  • Chicken bratwurst works with the same timing; cook it to 74°C (165°F).
  • Halal beef sausages of a similar thickness (such as Toulouse-style beef links) take about the same time. Thin links such as merguez or chipolatas take 9 to 11 minutes; see the chart.
  • Swap the peppers for 2 sliced courgettes or a handful of halved mushrooms, added at the same point.
  • No smoked paprika? Use sweet paprika with a pinch of chilli flakes.

Variations

  • Sumac onions: skip the peppers, cook only onions, then toss them with 1 tsp sumac and chopped parsley before piling onto the brats.
  • Harissa brats: brush the brats with 1 tbsp harissa mixed with 1 tsp oil when you turn them.
  • Bun-free plate: slice the cooked brats into coins and serve over rice or with flatbread and garlic yoghurt.
  • Za'atar rolls: brush the split rolls with olive oil and sprinkle with za'atar before toasting.

What to Serve With It

  • Serve in toasted rolls with mustard, the peppers and onions and a spoon of sauerkraut or pickled red cabbage.
  • Pair with roasted onion wedges and a green salad for a lighter plate.
  • Add potato wedges or a potato salad for a picnic-style dinner.
  • Slice into a pasta with tomato sauce or into scrambled eggs the next morning.

Storage

Cool cooked brats within 2 hours and refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4 days. They also freeze well for up to 2 months; wrap each one separately so you can take out what you need.

Reheating

Reheat refrigerated brats at 180°C (350°F) for 4 to 5 minutes, or from frozen for 8 to 10 minutes, until steaming hot in the centre. The peppers and onions reheat in 3 minutes at the same temperature.

Make Ahead

Slice and season the peppers and onions up to a day ahead and keep them covered in the fridge. Cook the brats fresh; they only take 15 minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you cook brats in an air fryer?

Yes. The air fryer browns bratwurst evenly on all sides and drains away excess fat. Cook raw brats at 190°C (375°F) for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once, until they reach 71°C (160°F) for beef or 74°C (165°F) for chicken.

How long do you cook brats in the air fryer at 400°F?

At 200°C (400°F), raw brats take about 10 to 12 minutes, turned halfway. That heat works but tends to split the casings on thick links, so 190°C (375°F) for 12 to 15 minutes gives juicier results with fewer bursts.

Should you poke holes in brats before air frying?

No. Piercing lets juice and fat run out, so the brats cook faster on the outside and end up drier. Cooking at 190°C (375°F) instead of a higher heat stops the casings splitting without any need for holes.

How do you know when brats are done in the air fryer?

Use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest end. Beef bratwurst is done at 71°C (160°F) and chicken bratwurst at 74°C (165°F). The casings should also be deep brown and tight, but colour and clear juices are not a reliable safety check on their own.

Can you cook frozen brats in the air fryer?

Yes, without thawing. Cook raw frozen brats at 180°C (350°F) for 10 minutes, turn them, then finish at 200°C (400°F) for 8 to 10 minutes. Precooked frozen brats need 10 to 12 minutes at 180°C. Check the centre temperature before serving.

Do you need to flip brats in the air fryer?

Turning once halfway gives even browning, because the side touching the basket gets less airflow. In an oven-style air fryer with racks, turn them twice and add 2 to 3 minutes to the total time.

Are brats better in the air fryer or the oven?

The air fryer is faster and browns better. Brats take 12 to 15 minutes in an air fryer against roughly 20 to 25 minutes in a conventional oven, and the strong fan crisps the casing more evenly than still oven air.
