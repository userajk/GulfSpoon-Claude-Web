Air Fryer Potatoes: Halved Baby Potatoes or Crispy Cubes

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: November 11, 2026
Prep 5 mins
Cook 18 mins
Total 23 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Halved baby potatoes roasted in the air fryer with olive oil, garlic and rosemary until the cut faces go deep golden and the insides turn creamy. The same method works for cubed large potatoes, and the chart covers whole, quartered and smashed.

Quick answer

Air fry halved baby potatoes at 200°C (400°F) for 15 to 18 minutes, or 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes of large potatoes for 18 to 22 minutes. Dry them, toss with 1 tablespoon of oil per 750g and shake every 6 to 7 minutes. They are done when the cut faces are deep golden and a knife slides in easily.

The quickest route to good air fryer potatoes is a bag of baby potatoes cut in half. The skins are thin enough to leave on and the waxy flesh turns creamy rather than dry. Each half also has a flat face that browns hard against the hot basket. They take 15 to 18 minutes at 200°C (400°F) with almost no knife work.

If all you have is large potatoes, cut them into even 2.5cm cubes and follow the same method: rinse, dry, oil, season, shake. Timings for every cut are in the chart, from whole mini potatoes to smashed. In Gulf supermarkets, baby potatoes often come in net bags with sizes from marble to almost 5cm. Spend a minute sorting them before you cut: leave the smallest whole, halve the middle sizes and quarter the biggest, so every piece is about the same thickness. For a sweeter side in the same basket, try diced sweet potato with smoked paprika or cinnamon.

How long do you cook potatoes in an air fryer?

Halved baby potatoes take 15 to 18 minutes at 200°C (400°F), and 2.5cm cubes of large potatoes take 18 to 22 minutes. Whole baby potatoes take longer, 20 to 25 minutes, because the skin slows moisture loss and heat has further to travel to the centre. That is also why whole ones come out creamy and soft while halves come out crisp.

Size matters more than variety. Every extra centimetre adds roughly 5 minutes. Baby gold potatoes are slightly drier and brown a touch faster; baby red potatoes are waxier and need a minute or two more before they feel tender.

Load is the other variable. Halves need a single layer. Cubes cope with two layers if you shake hard. Fill the basket to the brim and the top layer shields the bottom one, so you get pale, steamed potatoes underneath even after 30 minutes. For six people, two batches are faster in total than one crammed basket. Oven-style air fryers with trays sit the food further from the element, so add 3 to 5 minutes and swap the trays halfway.

For dinner from one machine, cook the potatoes first and keep them warm on a tray in a low oven, then use the hot basket for crispy-skinned air fryer chicken thighs or salmon fillets with crisp skin.

Which potatoes are best: baby, Yukon Gold or russet?

Any of them will cook well. They differ in texture, and in how well they survive being shaken.

  • Baby potatoes (gold, red or purple): young, waxy and thin-skinned. Creamy inside and crisp on the cut face, with no peeling. The least work and the most reliable batch.
  • Yukon Gold and other all-rounders: medium starch with a buttery interior. Cubes hold their shape, and the thin skin can stay on.
  • Russet and other floury potatoes (Maris Piper, King Edward): high starch, low moisture. Cubes roughen and crisp hard on the outside and turn fluffy inside, but the corners can break when shaken. The broken bits go extra crunchy.
  • Large red-skinned and other waxy potatoes: hold their shape but brown less and stay dense inside when cubed.

Loose potatoes in Gulf supermarkets are often labelled by country of origin rather than variety. To check, cut one in half and rub the faces together. If they stick and leave a white, starchy film, the potato is floury and will crisp well as cubes. If the faces feel wet and slide, it is waxy and better halved or cut into chunks.

Do you need to boil or soak potatoes before air frying?

No. Halved baby potatoes and 2.5cm cubes cook through in the air fryer alone. Rinsing cubes of large potatoes for 30 seconds is worth doing to wash off loose surface starch that otherwise glues them together and scorches. Long soaks matter more for thin fries than for cubes.

Boiling first is worth it in two cases. Parboiling cubes for 6 minutes, then steam-drying and shaking them in the colander, roughens the surface into a thicker, glassier crust like a Sunday roast potato and cuts the air fryer time to 15 to 18 minutes. Smashed potatoes need boiling, because the potato has to be fully tender before you flatten it.

How to make smashed baby potatoes: boil 750g whole baby potatoes in well-salted water for 12 to 15 minutes, until a knife slides in easily. Drain and steam dry for 5 minutes. Press each one with the base of a glass to about 1.5cm thick, so the skin splits and the edges go ragged. Brush with 2 tbsp olive oil, season, and air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 15 to 18 minutes without turning, until the edges are dark golden and crunchy. Lift them out with a fish slice.

Why are my air fryer potatoes not crispy?

Nearly every soft batch traces back to one of four things:

  1. Wet surfaces. Water has to evaporate before the surface can get past 100°C and start browning. Dry the potatoes in a towel.
  2. Overcrowding. Hot air needs a path around each piece. Single layer for halves, two layers at most for cubes.
  3. Halves face up. The cut face browns best against the preheated basket, so put halves cut side down.
  4. Salting too early. Salt draws water out. Season just before cooking, not 20 minutes ahead.

Whole baby potatoes are meant to be creamy with blistered skins rather than crunchy; halve them if you want crunch. Potatoes that are crisp at first but soften on the table are steaming in their own heat, so serve them in a wide, shallow dish and don't cover them. White smoke or a burnt smell early in cooking usually means old oil in the drawer, and our step-by-step air fryer cleaning guide covers how to clear it.

Air Fryer Potatoes

Scale:

Ingredients

Potatoes

  • 750 g baby potatoes (gold, red or mixed, about 3cm (1¼ in) across (1⅔ lb); or large Yukon Gold or russet potatoes, cubed)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • ¾ tsp fine salt
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp dried rosemary (crumbled, or 1 tbsp chopped fresh)
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika (optional)
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

To finish

  • 1 clove garlic (finely grated)
  • ½ lemon (juice only)
  • 1 tbsp fresh parsley (chopped)

Instructions

  1. 1

    Wash the potatoes and sort them by size. Halve any about 3cm (1¼ in) across, quarter any over 4cm and leave marble-sized ones whole, so all the pieces are roughly the same thickness.

    Using large potatoes? Cut them into 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes, swish them in cold water for 30 seconds to rinse off surface starch, then drain.

  2. 2

    Dry the potatoes thoroughly in a clean tea towel. The surfaces should feel tacky, not wet.

    Surface water has to boil off before anything browns, and in a closed basket it just steams the potatoes. Skipping this step is the usual reason for a soft batch.

  3. 3

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F) for 3 minutes.

  4. 4

    In a bowl, toss the potatoes with the oil until glossy, then add the salt, garlic powder, rosemary, smoked paprika and pepper and toss again.

  5. 5

    Tip them into the basket in a single layer, halves cut side down where you can. A 5 to 6 litre basket holds 750g of halves. Cubes can go two layers deep. In a 3 to 4 litre basket, cook in two batches.

  6. 6

    Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 15 to 18 minutes for halved baby potatoes, or 18 to 22 minutes for 2.5cm cubes. Shake the basket hard every 6 to 7 minutes.

  7. 7

    Test the largest piece: a knife should slide through the centre with no resistance, and the cut faces should be deep golden. If the outside is brown but the middle is firm, lower to 180°C (350°F) and give them 3 to 5 more minutes.

  8. 8

    Tip into a wide dish and toss with the grated garlic, lemon juice and parsley while hot. The heat takes the raw edge off the garlic without burning it. Serve straight away.

Equipment

Air fryer (5 to 6 litre basket) · Large mixing bowl · Clean tea towel · Sharp knife and chopping board

Tips

  • Sort a mixed bag by size before you start. A 2cm potato and a 5cm potato will never finish at the same time.
  • Leave the skins on. Skin browns faster than cut flesh and gives each piece a crisp edge.
  • Shake hard, not gently, so the pieces tumble and every face gets time against the hot air.
  • Add fresh garlic, parmesan and soft herbs such as parsley at the end. Over 15 minutes at 200°C they scorch. Rosemary is tough enough to go in at the start.
  • In summer, an air fryer adds far less heat to the kitchen than a full-size oven running for 45 minutes, which matters in a Gulf flat in August.

Troubleshooting

  • Brown outside, hard in the middle: pieces too big for the time or the heat too high. Lower to 180°C (350°F) for 3 to 5 more minutes, and cut smaller next time.
  • Pale and soft all over: too wet or too crowded. Dry the potatoes and cook in two batches.
  • Pale cut faces on halves: they were face up or the basket was not preheated. Turn them cut side down in a hot basket.
  • Smashed potatoes broke apart: under-boiled, or smashed while wet. Boil until fully tender and steam dry before flattening.
  • Garlic tasted bitter: fresh garlic went in at the start. Use garlic powder during cooking and fresh garlic after.

Substitutions

  • Swap dried rosemary for dried thyme, oregano or 1 tsp za'atar.
  • Use ghee or melted butter instead of oil for a richer flavour, and drop to 190°C (375°F) because the milk solids brown fast.
  • No baby potatoes? Cut Yukon Gold or other waxy potatoes into 3cm chunks and cook as for halves.
  • Replace the lemon with a pinch of sumac for acidity.

Variations

  • Batata harra style: toss the cooked potatoes with 2 cloves of grated garlic fried in 1 tbsp olive oil, ½ tsp chilli flakes, a handful of chopped coriander and a squeeze of lemon.
  • Garlic parmesan: toss the hot potatoes with 2 tbsp finely grated parmesan and ½ tsp garlic powder so the cheese melts onto the skins.
  • Za'atar and lemon: replace the rosemary with 1½ tsp za'atar and finish with lemon zest and extra virgin olive oil.
  • Harissa: mix 1 tbsp harissa paste into the oil before tossing and cook at 190°C (375°F) so the paste doesn't scorch.
  • Potatoes, onions and peppers: add 1 red onion and 1 pepper, both cut to 2.5cm, for the final 10 minutes.

What to Serve With It

  • With grilled chicken, a chopped cucumber and tomato salad and garlic toum.
  • Alongside fried eggs and grilled halloumi for a weekend breakfast.
  • As a warm potato salad: toss halves with spring onion, capers, dill and a mustard and olive oil dressing.
  • On a mezze table with olives, labneh and flatbread, or as an easy iftar side.

Storage

Cool completely, then refrigerate in an airtight container lined with paper towel for up to 4 days. To freeze, spread cooled potatoes on a tray until firm, then bag them for up to 2 months. Whole baby potatoes freeze better than cut ones.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 190°C (375°F) for 4 to 6 minutes, shaking once, until the edges crisp again. Halve cold whole potatoes first. From frozen, add 4 to 6 minutes. A microwave warms them but leaves them soft.

Make Ahead

Halve or cube the potatoes up to 24 hours ahead and keep them submerged in cold water in the fridge. Drain, dry thoroughly and season just before cooking. Smashed potatoes can be boiled and flattened up to 2 days ahead, refrigerated on a tray and air fried from cold with 2 to 3 extra minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to cook potatoes in the air fryer?

At 200°C (400°F), halved baby potatoes take 15 to 18 minutes and 2.5cm cubes of large potatoes take 18 to 22 minutes. Whole baby potatoes need 20 to 25 minutes. Shake every 6 to 7 minutes and test with a knife: it should slide into the centre with no resistance.

What temperature should I air fry potatoes at?

Use 200°C (400°F). At that heat the outside browns in about the time the centre takes to soften, whereas lower temperatures dry potatoes out before they colour. If your machine runs hot or you add a paste such as harissa, drop to 190°C (375°F) and add 2 to 3 minutes.

How long do small potatoes take in the air fryer?

Small or mini potatoes of 2 to 2.5cm take 15 to 18 minutes whole at 200°C (400°F). Baby potatoes of about 3cm take 15 to 18 minutes halved or 20 to 25 minutes whole. Shake the basket every 6 to 7 minutes.

Do you have to boil potatoes before putting them in the air fryer?

No. Halved baby potatoes and 2.5cm cubes cook through from raw. Boil first only for smashed potatoes, or parboil cubes for 6 minutes if you want a thicker, crunchier crust and a shorter air fryer time.

Do you have to flip potatoes in the air fryer?

Shake or toss them every 6 to 7 minutes. Pieces on the bottom get less airflow and brown more slowly, so moving them around gives even colour. Smashed potatoes are the exception: leave them flat and unturned.

Why are my air fryer potatoes hard?

Hard centres mean the pieces were too large for the cooking time or the basket was overloaded. Lower the heat to 180°C (350°F) and cook 3 to 5 minutes more so the middles soften without burning the outside. Cut to even sizes next time.

Do you need to peel potatoes for the air fryer?

No. Baby potato skins are thin and crisp nicely, and scrubbed skin on large potatoes helps cubes hold together. Peel only if the skin is thick or damaged, and always cut away green patches.

How long do air fryer potatoes last in the fridge?

Up to 4 days in an airtight container lined with paper towel. Reheat in the air fryer at 190°C (375°F) for 4 to 6 minutes to bring the crisp edges back.
