Air Fryer Chocolate Chip Cookies: Small-Batch in 8 Minutes

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: September 24, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 8 mins
Total 33 mins
Servings 6
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Small-batch chocolate chip cookies baked in the air fryer — crispy golden edges, soft and gooey centers, and melted chocolate in every bite. This recipe makes just 6 cookies: perfect for a quick dessert without the temptation of three dozen cooling on the counter. No oven preheating, no baking sheets. Ready in 8 minutes.

The air fryer makes surprisingly excellent cookies — and the case for baking them this way is not just speed (though 8 minutes versus 12 to 15 in an oven is nice). The real advantage is texture. The circulating hot air crisps the edges of the cookie while the center stays soft and slightly underdone, creating that bakery-style contrast between crunch and chew that home ovens often struggle to achieve.

The other advantage is portion control. This is a small-batch recipe that makes exactly 6 cookies. No scaling up a giant recipe and ending up with three dozen cookies that call to you from the kitchen counter all week. You mix the dough, scoop it, and eight minutes later you have a warm dessert — enough for two people with a couple left over, or enough for one person who has had a day.

The dough itself is a straightforward brown-butter chocolate chip cookie base. Browning the butter takes two extra minutes but adds a nutty, caramel-like depth that separates these from basic cookies. If you are in a rush, melted (not browned) butter works fine — the cookies will be less complex in flavor but still excellent.

Pro Tip: Chill the dough balls for 15 minutes in the fridge before air frying. Cold dough spreads more slowly, producing thicker cookies with better texture.

Air Fryer Chocolate Chip Cookies

Scale:

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter (browned or melted)
  • ¼ cup brown sugar (packed — dark or light)
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • ½ cup all-purpose flour (plus 2 tbsp (about 80g))
  • ¼ tsp baking soda
  • ¼ tsp salt
  • ⅓ cup chocolate chips (semi-sweet or dark)
  • flaky sea salt (for finishing — optional)

Instructions

  1. 1

    If browning the butter: melt 3 tablespoons of butter in a small saucepan over medium heat. Swirl occasionally until the butter foams, turns golden, and smells nutty — about 3 minutes. Pour into a mixing bowl and let cool for 5 minutes.

    Watch the butter closely once it starts to foam. It goes from browned to burnt in seconds. The brown bits at the bottom are flavor, not burned — scrape them all into the bowl.

  2. 2

    Stir the brown sugar and granulated sugar into the cooled butter until combined. Add the egg yolk and vanilla extract and mix until smooth.

  3. 3

    Add the flour, baking soda, and salt. Stir with a spatula until just combined — do not overmix. Fold in the chocolate chips.

  4. 4

    Scoop the dough into 6 equal balls (about 1.5 tablespoons each). Place on a small plate and chill in the fridge for 15 minutes.

    Chilling is optional but recommended. Cold dough spreads more slowly, producing thicker, chewier cookies with better shape.

  5. 5

    Preheat the air fryer to 165°C (325°F). Cut a piece of parchment paper to fit the basket.

  6. 6

    Place the dough balls on the parchment in the air fryer basket, leaving at least 5cm (2 inches) between each cookie. You may need to bake in two batches of 3.

  7. 7

    Air fry at 165°C (325°F) for 7 to 8 minutes. The cookies should look slightly underdone in the center — they will firm up as they cool.

    Pull them at 7 minutes if you want gooey centers. The carry-over heat continues cooking them for another minute after removal.

  8. 8

    Sprinkle with flaky sea salt immediately. Let the cookies cool on the parchment for 3 minutes (they will be very soft at first), then transfer to a plate. Serve warm.

Tips

  • Use parchment paper — always. Cookie dough will stick to the air fryer basket and burn.
  • Lower temperature is key. Unlike other air fryer recipes that use 200°C, cookies need 165°C (325°F). Higher temps burn the edges before the center sets.
  • The cookies look underdone when you pull them out. That is correct. They firm up significantly as they cool. If they look done in the air fryer, they are overdone.
  • Chilling the dough prevents flat, thin cookies. Even 10 minutes in the fridge helps.
  • Do not overcrowd. The cookies spread, and touching cookies merge into one giant cookie (delicious but not the goal).
  • Flaky sea salt on top is optional but highly recommended — it intensifies the chocolate and caramel notes from the brown butter.

Substitutions

  • No time to brown butter? Melted butter works fine. The cookies will be good, just less complex in flavor.
  • Use 1 whole egg instead of the yolk for a cakier, puffier cookie. The yolk alone produces a richer, chewier result.
  • Swap chocolate chips for chopped dark chocolate bars for a more artisanal look and better melt.
  • Add 2 tablespoons of chopped nuts (pecans or walnuts) for a crunchy variation.
  • For gluten-free cookies, use a 1:1 gluten-free flour blend in the same amount.

Storage

Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Reheat in the air fryer at 150°C (300°F) for 2 minutes to restore the warm, gooey center. Raw dough balls can be frozen for up to 3 months — bake straight from frozen, adding 1 to 2 minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you bake cookies in an air fryer?

Absolutely. The air fryer produces excellent cookies — crispy edges and soft centers — in about 8 minutes at 165°C (325°F). Use parchment paper and leave space between each cookie for spreading.

Why are my air fryer cookies burnt on the edges?

The temperature is too high. Cookies need 165°C (325°F), not the 200°C used for most air fryer cooking. Also check that you are using parchment paper — direct contact with the metal basket scorches the bottom.

How long do cookies take in the air fryer?

7 to 8 minutes at 165°C (325°F). They should look slightly underdone when removed — they firm up as they cool. If they look fully done in the air fryer, they will be hard once cooled.

Why did my cookies spread too flat?

The dough was too warm. Chill the dough balls for 15 minutes before baking. Also make sure you used the right flour measurement — too little flour causes spreading.

Can I use store-bought cookie dough?

Yes. Slice pre-made cookie dough into rounds and air fry at 165°C for 6 to 8 minutes. Results are good, though not as good as fresh dough.
