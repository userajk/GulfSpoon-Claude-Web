31:T49e,The quickest route to good air fryer potatoes is a bag of baby potatoes cut in half. The skins are thin enough to leave on and the waxy flesh turns creamy rather than dry. Each half also has a flat face that browns hard against the hot basket. They take 15 to 18 minutes at 200°C (400°F) with almost no knife work.

If all you have is large potatoes, cut them into even 2.5cm cubes and follow the same method: rinse, dry, oil, season, shake. Timings for every cut are in the chart, from whole mini potatoes to smashed. For thick, fluffy-centred spears for dipping, see our [seasoned air fryer potato wedges](/recipes/air-fryer-potato-wedges/), and for a whole spud with a split top and toppings, our [air fryer baked potato with crispy salted skin](/recipes/air-fryer-baked-potato/).

In Gulf supermarkets, baby potatoes often come in net bags with sizes from marble to almost 5cm. Spend a minute sorting them before you cut: leave the smallest whole, halve the middle sizes and quarter the biggest, so every piece is about the same thickness. For a sweeter side in the same basket, try [diced sweet potato with smoked paprika or cinnamon](/recipes/air-fryer-sweet-potato-cubes/).32:T4f4,Halved baby potatoes take 15 to 18 minutes at 200°C (400°F), and 2.5cm cubes of large potatoes take 18 to 22 minutes. Whole baby potatoes take longer, 20 to 25 minutes, because the skin slows moisture loss and heat has further to travel to the centre. That is also why whole ones come out creamy and soft while halves come out crisp.

Size matters more than variety. Every extra centimetre adds roughly 5 minutes. Baby gold potatoes are slightly drier and brown a touch faster; baby red potatoes are waxier and need a minute or two more before they feel tender.

Load is the other variable. Halves need a single layer. Cubes cope with two layers if you shake hard. Fill the basket to the brim and the top layer shields the bottom one, so you get pale, steamed potatoes underneath even after 30 minutes. For six people, two batches are faster in total than one crammed basket. Oven-style air fryers with trays sit the food further from the element, so add 3 to 5 minutes and swap the trays halfway.

For dinner from one machine, cook the potatoes first and keep them warm on a tray in a low oven, then use the hot basket for [crispy-skinned air fryer chicken thighs](/recipes/air-fryer-chicken-thighs/) or [salmon fillets with crisp skin](/recipes/air-fryer-salmon/).33:T43f,Any of them will cook well. They differ in texture, and in how well they survive being shaken.

- **Baby potatoes (gold, red or purple):** young, waxy and thin-skinned. Creamy inside and crisp on the cut face, with no peeling. The least work and the most reliable batch.
- **Yukon Gold and other all-rounders:** medium starch with a buttery interior. Cubes hold their shape, and the thin skin can stay on.
- **Russet and other floury potatoes (Maris Piper, King Edward):** high starch, low moisture. Cubes roughen and crisp hard on the outside and turn fluffy inside, but the corners can break when shaken. The broken bits go extra crunchy.
- **Large red-skinned and other waxy potatoes:** hold their shape but brown less and stay dense inside when cubed.

Loose potatoes in Gulf supermarkets are often labelled by country of origin rather than variety. To check, cut one in half and rub the faces together. If they stick and leave a white, starchy film, the potato is floury and will crisp well as cubes. If the faces feel wet and slide, it is waxy and better halved or cut into chunks.34:T44e,No. Halved baby potatoes and 2.5cm cubes cook through in the air fryer alone. Rinsing cubes of large potatoes for 30 seconds is worth doing to wash off loose surface starch that otherwise glues them together and scorches. Long soaks matter more for thin fries than for cubes.

Boiling first is worth it in two cases. **Parboiling cubes** for 6 minutes, then steam-drying and shaking them in the colander, roughens the surface into a thicker, glassier crust like a Sunday roast potato and cuts the air fryer time to 15 to 18 minutes. **Smashed potatoes** need boiling, because the potato has to be fully tender before you flatten it.

**How to make smashed baby potatoes:** boil 750g whole baby potatoes in well-salted water for 12 to 15 minutes, until a knife slides in easily. Drain and steam dry for 5 minutes. Press each one with the base of a glass to about 1.5cm thick, so the skin splits and the edges go ragged. Brush with 2 tbsp olive oil, season, and air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 15 to 18 minutes without turning, until the edges are dark golden and crunchy. Lift them out with a fish slice.35:T419,Raw brats take 12 to 15 minutes at 190°C (375°F), turned once halfway. Thickness decides where you land in that range: a 3cm link is usually done at 13 minutes, a fat 3.5cm butcher's brat closer to 15. Chicken bratwurst cooks in about the same time as beef but must reach 74°C (165°F) rather than 71°C (160°F), so check it with a thermometer rather than going by colour.

At 200°C (400°F) raw brats are ready in 10 to 12 minutes, which is why many recipes use it. On thicker links, though, the casing tightens faster than the filling expands and often splits along one side, and juice runs out through the split. At 190°C that rarely happens.

Leave space between them. Brats that touch stay pale where they meet, because the hot air can't reach that strip of casing.

Oven-style air fryers with racks brown more slowly on the underside, so turn the brats twice instead of once and add 2 to 3 minutes. For more on why spacing and fan speed matter, see [how an air fryer actually cooks food](/beyond-the-recipes/how-does-an-air-fryer-work/).36:T568,**From frozen:** don't thaw them. Start raw frozen brats at 180°C (350°F) for 10 minutes, so the centre thaws before the casing over-browns, then turn them, raise the heat to 200°C (400°F) and cook for another 8 to 10 minutes. If the links are frozen together, run the pack under cold water for 30 seconds and prise them apart with a butter knife. Always confirm the centre temperature, because frozen brats vary more than fresh ones.

**Precooked or smoked brats** are already safe to eat, so you're only reheating and browning them: 6 to 8 minutes at 190°C (375°F) until they're steaming hot in the middle.

**Simmered first:** if you like a very juicy brat with a softer bite, simmer raw brats for 10 minutes in salted water or chicken stock with a sliced onion, keeping it just below a boil so the casings don't burst. Drain, pat them dry, then air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 4 to 6 minutes to brown the outside. Simmering is optional; the straight air-fryer method cooks them through safely on its own.

If you'd rather skip the peppers, cook the brats over thick [roasted onion wedges](/recipes/air-fryer-onions/) instead, using the same 5-minute head start. Beef brats leave a film of fat in the drawer either way; wipe it out while it's still warm, and see our [air fryer cleaning guide](/beyond-the-recipes/how-to-clean-air-fryer/) for grease that has already baked on.37:T414,<p>Potato wedges are a different job from roast potatoes. You want a thick piece with real fluffy potato inside, two flat cut faces that crisp, and a strip of skin along the back that goes chewy and slightly crunchy. That shape only works if every wedge is the same thickness, so most of the method here is about cutting.</p>
<p>Floury potatoes such as russet, Maris Piper or King Edward make the best wedges because their high starch content cooks up dry and fluffy. A quick rinse and a thorough dry stop them steaming, and a teaspoon of cornflour on top of the oil gives the faces a thin, crackly shell. At 200°C (400°F) they take 18 to 22 minutes.</p>
