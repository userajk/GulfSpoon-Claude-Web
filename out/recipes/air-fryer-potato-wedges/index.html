Air Fryer Potato Wedges with Smoky Seasoning

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: November 23, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 22 mins
Total 32 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Russet potato wedges tossed in oil, cornflour and a smoked paprika and garlic seasoning, then air fried until the cut faces crisp and the centres turn fluffy. Cut evenly, turned once, and served with a choice of four quick dips.

Quick answer

Cook potato wedges at 200°C (400°F) for 18 to 22 minutes, turning once at halfway. Cut each large potato into 8 wedges about 2cm (¾ inch) thick at the back, rinse and dry them, then toss with 1 tablespoon of oil and 1 teaspoon of cornflour. They are done when both cut faces are golden and crisp.

Potato wedges are a different job from roast potatoes. You want a thick piece with real fluffy potato inside, two flat cut faces that crisp, and a strip of skin along the back that goes chewy and slightly crunchy. That shape only works if every wedge is the same thickness, so most of the method here is about cutting.

Floury potatoes such as russet, Maris Piper or King Edward make the best wedges because their high starch content cooks up dry and fluffy. A quick rinse and a thorough dry stop them steaming, and a teaspoon of cornflour on top of the oil gives the faces a thin, crackly shell. At 200°C (400°F) they take 18 to 22 minutes.

For smaller pieces with more crust and less fluffy centre, our halved baby potatoes and roasted potato cubes use the same basket and temperature. For the sweet version, our sweet potato fries recipe covers thinner cuts of orange-fleshed sweet potato, which brown faster and need less time.

How do you cut potato wedges for the air fryer?

Cut every wedge to the same thickness at its widest point, measured across the skin. Thickness sets the cooking time far more than length or variety does.

  1. Halve the potato lengthways.
  2. Lay each half flat and cut it lengthways into 4, angling the knife so each wedge meets at the centre line. That gives 8 wedges about 2cm thick from a 300g potato.
  3. For a smaller potato of around 200g, cut each half into 3 instead of 4.
  4. For thin wedges, cut each half into 6. For chunky wedges, cut each half into 3 from a large potato.

Keep the skin on. It holds each wedge together when you turn it and gives the back a chewy, savoury edge. Scrub it well and cut away any green patches or deep eyes.

How long to cook potato wedges in the air fryer

Standard wedges of 2cm take 18 to 22 minutes at 200°C (400°F). Thin 1.2cm wedges take 14 to 16 minutes and chunky 3cm wedges take 22 to 26 minutes. Turn them once, from one cut face to the other, rather than shaking: shaking breaks the thin edges and leaves some wedges lying on their skin, where they brown less.

Frozen shop-bought wedges go straight in without thawing. Cook them at 200°C (400°F) for 14 to 18 minutes, shaking once at about 7 minutes. They are already par-fried, so they need no extra oil. Our frozen food air fryer cooking times chart covers converting other package instructions.

Parboiling is optional. Boiling the wedges for 5 minutes, then steam-drying them for 5, gives a rougher surface that crisps more and cuts the air fryer time to 14 to 17 minutes. Handle them gently, because parboiled russet wedges break easily.

Why are my potato wedges soft or hard in the middle?

Soft, pale wedges are almost always wet or crowded. Water on the surface turns to steam before the potato can brown, and wedges packed against each other trap that steam. Dry them in a towel, keep to one layer, and cook in two batches if needed. A waxy potato will also give a denser, less crisp wedge than a floury one.

Hard centres mean the wedges were too thick for the time or the heat was too high, so the outside coloured before the inside cooked. Drop to 180°C (350°F) and give them 4 to 5 minutes more. Next time cut them thinner or use the chunky-wedge time.

Burnt seasoning comes from fresh garlic or sugary spice blends added at the start. Use garlic powder during cooking and add fresh garlic or grated cheese only in the last 2 minutes.

What dip goes with potato wedges?

Each of these takes under 5 minutes to stir together:

  • Garlic yoghurt: 150g thick yoghurt, 1 grated garlic clove, 1 tbsp lemon juice, a pinch of salt and 1 tsp dried mint.
  • Spicy mayo: 4 tbsp mayonnaise with 1 to 2 tsp sriracha or harissa and a squeeze of lime.
  • Tahini lemon: 3 tbsp tahini, 2 tbsp lemon juice, 3 to 4 tbsp cold water whisked in until smooth, salt and a pinch of cumin.
  • Quick cheese sauce: melt 60g grated cheddar into 80ml warm milk with ½ tsp cornflour, stirring until it thickens.

Wedges make a natural side for crispy air fryer chicken tenders or halal beef bratwurst with peppers and onions, and with a couple of dips they turn into a sharing plate for a film night.

Air Fryer Potato Wedges

Scale:

Ingredients

Wedges

  • 2 large russet or other floury potatoes (about 300g (10 oz) each, scrubbed)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil or sunflower oil
  • 1 tsp cornflour (cornstarch)

Seasoning

  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • ½ tsp onion powder
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • ¾ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Instructions

  1. 1

    Halve each potato lengthways. Lay each half cut side down and cut it lengthways into 4 wedges, angling the knife towards the centre so every wedge has skin along its back. You should have 8 wedges per potato, about 2cm (¾ inch) thick at the widest point.

    If one end of the potato is much thinner, cut the wedges from that end slightly wider so they finish with the rest.

  2. 2

    Put the wedges in a bowl of cold water and swish for 30 seconds until the water clouds. Drain.

  3. 3

    Dry the wedges thoroughly in a clean tea towel, including the skin side. The faces should feel tacky, not wet.

  4. 4

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F) for 3 minutes. Mix the seasoning in a small bowl.

  5. 5

    Toss the wedges with the oil until every face is glossy. Sprinkle over the cornflour and toss until no white powder shows, then add the seasoning and toss again.

    Oil first, then cornflour. Cornflour on a dry surface clumps into chalky patches.

  6. 6

    Lay the wedges in the basket on one cut face, in a single layer with small gaps. A 5 to 6 litre basket fits about 12 to 16 wedges, so cook 2 potatoes in one or two batches depending on your basket.

  7. 7

    Air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 10 minutes, then turn each wedge onto its other cut face with tongs. Cook for 8 to 12 minutes more.

  8. 8

    They are done when both cut faces are golden and crisp, the skin has darkened and wrinkled slightly, and a knife pushed into the thickest part meets no resistance. Serve straight away with your choice of dip.

Equipment

Air fryer (5 to 6 litre basket) · Large chef's knife · Mixing bowl · Clean tea towel · Tongs

Tips

  • Measure one wedge before you cut the rest. Matching the first one by eye keeps the batch even.
  • Lay wedges on a cut face, not on their skin. Both cut faces get direct contact with the hot basket over the cook.
  • Turn with tongs rather than shaking, which snaps the thin edges.
  • Salt again lightly as soon as they come out. The surface is still slightly oily and the salt sticks.
  • In an oven-style air fryer, use the upper rack for the first half and swap trays when you turn the wedges.

Troubleshooting

  • Soft and pale: wet or crowded. Dry the wedges in a towel and cook in batches.
  • Hard in the middle: too thick for the time. Drop to 180°C (350°F) for 4 to 5 more minutes.
  • Thin ends burnt: the potato tapers. Cut wedges from the narrow end wider, or trim the tips.
  • Wedges broke when turned: they were parboiled too long or shaken. Turn with tongs and parboil for 5 minutes at most.
  • Chalky patches: cornflour went on before the oil. Oil first, then cornflour.

Substitutions

  • No cornflour? Use potato starch, or leave it out for a slightly softer surface.
  • Swap the oregano for dried thyme, rosemary or 1 tsp za'atar.
  • Yukon Gold potatoes give creamier, slightly less fluffy wedges and cook 2 minutes faster.
  • Use ghee instead of oil for a richer flavour, and drop to 190°C (375°F).

Variations

  • Garlic parmesan: toss the hot wedges with 3 tbsp finely grated parmesan, ½ tsp garlic powder and chopped parsley.
  • Cajun: replace the seasoning with 2 tsp Cajun spice and a pinch of brown sugar.
  • Za'atar and sumac: season with 1½ tsp za'atar before cooking and finish with ½ tsp sumac and olive oil.
  • Masala: use ½ tsp each of chilli powder, ground cumin and turmeric, then finish with chaat masala and lemon.
  • Loaded wedges: top with grated cheddar for the last 2 minutes, then add chopped halal turkey bacon, spring onion and a spoonful of garlic yoghurt.

What to Serve With It

  • With halal beef burgers instead of fries.
  • Alongside grilled chicken and a crunchy coleslaw.
  • Next to air fryer salmon or chicken thighs, with the tahini lemon dip doubling as the sauce.
  • With fried eggs and grilled tomatoes for a late breakfast.

Storage

Refrigerate cooled wedges in an airtight container lined with paper towel for up to 4 days. To freeze, spread them on a tray until firm, then bag for up to 2 months.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 190°C (375°F) for 4 to 6 minutes until the faces crisp again. From frozen, allow 8 to 10 minutes. The microwave leaves them soft.

Make Ahead

Cut the wedges up to 24 hours ahead and keep them submerged in cold water in the fridge so they don't discolour. Drain, dry thoroughly and season just before cooking.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do potato wedges take in the air fryer?

Standard 2cm wedges take 18 to 22 minutes at 200°C (400°F), turned once at 10 minutes. Thin 1.2cm wedges take 14 to 16 minutes and chunky 3cm wedges take 22 to 26 minutes. They are done when a knife slides into the thickest part.

What temperature do you cook potato wedges at in the air fryer?

Use 200°C (400°F). It crisps the cut faces while the centre steams to a fluffy texture. If the outsides darken before the middles soften, finish at 180°C (350°F) for a few minutes.

Do you need to boil potato wedges before air frying?

No. Raw 2cm wedges cook through in 18 to 22 minutes. Parboiling for 5 minutes is optional: it roughens the surface for a crunchier crust and cuts the air fryer time to 14 to 17 minutes, but the wedges break more easily.

Should you soak potato wedges before air frying?

A 30-second rinse is enough. It washes off loose surface starch that would otherwise make the wedges stick together and scorch. Long soaks add little for thick wedges, and whatever you do, dry them thoroughly before oiling.

How do you cook frozen potato wedges in the air fryer?

Cook them straight from frozen at 200°C (400°F) for 14 to 18 minutes, shaking once at about 7 minutes. Shop-bought wedges are already par-fried, so they need no extra oil. Spread them in a single layer.

Are potato wedges better in the oven or the air fryer?

The air fryer gives crisper wedges in less time. A 2cm wedge takes about 20 minutes with no oven preheat, and the strong fan dries the surface faster. An oven is better for large batches that would not fit in one or two baskets.

Why are my potato wedges not crispy in the air fryer?

Usually the wedges went in wet, were packed too tightly, or were lying on their skin. Dry them well, toss in oil then cornflour, lay them on a cut face in a single layer and turn once at halfway.
