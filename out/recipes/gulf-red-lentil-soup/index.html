Shorbat Adas: Gulf Red Lentil Soup with Cumin and Lemon

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: July 25, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 25 mins
Total 35 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine Levantine

Shorbat adas — lentil soup — is one of those dishes that belongs to everyone and no one. You will find versions of it from Cairo to Karachi, from Istanbul to Aden. But the Gulf version has its own identity, shaped by centuries of trade and the practical realities of cooking in a region where fresh produce was historically scarce and pantry staples had to travel well. Lentils, dried spices, onions, and lemons — everything in this soup could survive a long journey by dhow or caravan.

The dish has roots that go back thousands of years. Lentils are one of the oldest cultivated crops in human history, domesticated in the Fertile Crescent around 8,000 BCE. The biblical story of Esau selling his birthright for a bowl of lentil stew speaks to how central this humble legume was to the ancient Near East. Arab traders carried lentils and the tradition of lentil soups across the peninsula, and by the medieval period, cookbooks from Baghdad and Damascus included dozens of lentil preparations.

In the Gulf today, shorbat adas holds a particular place during Ramadan. After a full day of fasting, the body needs something gentle and nourishing to break the fast — not a heavy meal that shocks the stomach. A bowl of warm lentil soup does exactly that. It is light enough to ease you back into eating, rich enough to begin restoring energy, and quick enough that even a tired cook can have it ready before the maghrib prayer call. Most families serve it alongside dates and water as the first course of iftar, before the main dishes come out.

What separates the Gulf version from its cousins elsewhere is the finishing. A drizzle of good olive oil, a hard squeeze of fresh lemon, and sometimes a sprinkle of sumac or a scattering of fried garlic chips on top. The cumin is toasted until it smells almost smoky. The soup is usually blended smooth, not left chunky — Gulf palates tend to prefer it silky.

A velvety, golden soup made from split red lentils simmered with cumin, turmeric, garlic, and a generous squeeze of lemon. This is the soup that appears on nearly every iftar table during Ramadan and on cold-weather dinner tables the rest of the year. Simple enough for a weeknight, satisfying enough to build a meal around.

Gulf Red Lentil Soup (Shorbat Adas)

Scale:

Ingredients

Soup Base

  • 1 cups split red lentils (about 300g, rinsed until water runs clear)
  • 1 large yellow onion (finely diced)
  • 3 garlic cloves (minced)
  • 1 medium carrot (peeled and diced — adds sweetness)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp ground turmeric
  • ½ tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp salt (or to taste)
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • 5 cups water or vegetable broth (about 1.2 liters)

To Finish

  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice (about 1 lemon)
  • 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil (for drizzling)
  • ¼ tsp ground cumin (for garnish)

Instructions

  1. 1

    Rinse the red lentils in a fine-mesh sieve under cold running water until the water runs clear. This removes surface starch and prevents the soup from getting gummy.

  2. 2

    Heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook, stirring occasionally, until soft and translucent — about 4 to 5 minutes. Do not brown the onion; you want sweetness, not caramelization.

  3. 3

    Add the garlic, cumin, turmeric, and coriander. Stir for about 30 seconds until the spices are fragrant and coat the onion.

    Toasting the spices in oil before adding liquid blooms their essential oils and deepens the flavor significantly.

  4. 4

    Add the rinsed lentils, diced carrot, salt, pepper, and 5 cups of water or broth. Bring to a boil over high heat, then reduce to a gentle simmer. Partially cover the pot.

  5. 5

    Simmer for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils have completely broken down and the carrot is very soft. The soup will thicken as it cooks. If it gets too thick, add a splash of water.

    Red lentils fall apart on their own — that is what you want. No soaking needed.

  6. 6

    Use an immersion blender to puree the soup until smooth and velvety. If using a countertop blender, let the soup cool slightly first and blend in batches with the lid slightly ajar to let steam escape.

  7. 7

    Taste and adjust salt. Stir in 2 tablespoons of fresh lemon juice — this brightens the whole soup and balances the earthiness of the lentils.

  8. 8

    Ladle into bowls. Drizzle each serving with a thread of extra-virgin olive oil and a pinch of cumin. Serve with lemon wedges on the side and warm flatbread.

Tips

  • Always rinse red lentils before cooking. The starchy residue on the surface can make the soup gluey instead of silky.
  • The lemon juice should go in at the end, not during cooking. Acid slows down the softening of lentils and can extend your cook time if added too early.
  • For a richer flavor, use chicken or vegetable broth instead of water. But even with plain water and good spices, this soup tastes excellent.
  • If you like a thinner consistency, add more broth after blending. If you prefer it thick and porridge-like (as some Gulf families do), use only 4 cups of liquid.

Substitutions

  • No red lentils? Yellow split lentils work the same way. Green or brown lentils hold their shape and will not produce a smooth soup.
  • No immersion blender? A potato masher will give you a rustic, semi-chunky texture that is also traditional in some households.
  • Replace carrot with a small potato for a thicker, heartier version.
  • Add a diced tomato with the onion for a slightly tangier, more Egyptian-style version.

Storage

Keeps well in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The soup will thicken as it cools — add a splash of water or broth when reheating. Freezes well for up to 3 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to soak red lentils before cooking?

No. Red lentils are split and cook very quickly — 20 to 25 minutes with no soaking. Just rinse them to remove starch.

Can I make this soup in a pressure cooker or Instant Pot?

Yes. Sauté the onion and spices using the sauté function, then add lentils, carrot, and liquid. Pressure cook on high for 8 minutes with a natural release. Blend and finish with lemon as directed.

Why does my lentil soup taste flat?

Two things usually fix a flat lentil soup: more salt and more lemon juice. Lentils absorb a lot of salt, so you often need more than you expect. And the acidity from lemon is essential — it lifts the entire flavor.

Is shorbat adas healthy?

Very. Red lentils are high in protein, iron, and fiber. A bowl of this soup has roughly 230 calories and 16 grams of protein. With no dairy or oil beyond the finishing drizzle, it is naturally light.
