Crispy Air Fryer Chickpeas from a Can

By Sarah Al-Fayed|Updated: November 15, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 18 mins
Total 28 mins
Servings 2
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Canned chickpeas roasted in the air fryer until they rattle in the basket and crunch all the way through. They go in plain and only get the oil and spices for the last few minutes, so the centres dry out before the seasoning can scorch.

Quick answer

Air fry rinsed, well-dried canned chickpeas at 200°C (400°F) for 15 to 18 minutes: 10 minutes plain, then toss with oil and spices and cook 5 to 8 minutes more, shaking twice. They are done when they rattle like gravel and a broken one is dry and crumbly inside, not creamy.

Most recipes for air fryer chickpeas toss everything in oil and spices at the start and cook for 12 to 15 minutes. That gives you chickpeas that are crisp on the outside for about ten minutes, then turn leathery as the moisture trapped inside works its way out. Paprika and garlic powder also go bitter when they sit at 200°C for a quarter of an hour.

This method splits the cook in two. The chickpeas go in plain for 10 minutes, which drives off surface water and starts drying the centre. Then they get a tablespoon of oil and the spice mix and go back in for 5 to 8 minutes, long enough to toast the spices and brown the outside. Cooked this way they crunch right through and hold up for most of a day.

One 400g (14 oz) can makes about a cup and a half, enough for two as a snack or four as a salad topping. Scatter them over a bowl of Gulf red lentil soup, toss them into a grain bowl with crispy air fryer tofu, or eat them warm from the basket.

Why are my air fryer chickpeas not crunchy?

Water is almost always the reason. A canned chickpea is soft, cooked and well over half water by weight. The crunch only comes when enough of that water has left, and it can only leave through the skin. If you oil the chickpeas at the start, the oil seals the surface, the outside browns fast and the inside stays damp. Within an hour that moisture migrates outwards and the crust goes chewy.

Three other things cause soft chickpeas:

  • Too many in the basket. A single 400g can fills a 5 to 6 litre basket in one layer. Two cans piled up steam each other, so cook them in two batches.
  • Pulling them too early. Golden brown is not the same as done. Break one open: if the centre is creamy, they need longer.
  • Storing them warm in a sealed jar. Trapped steam softens everything. Cool them completely on an open plate first.

If you live somewhere humid, as much of the Gulf coast is from June to September, chickpeas pick up moisture from the air faster. Cook the extra-crunchy version at 180°C (350°F) for 22 to 25 minutes if you plan to keep them for a day or two.

Why do chickpeas pop in the air fryer?

Chickpeas pop when water trapped under the skin turns to steam faster than it can escape, and the skin bursts. It happens most in the first 5 to 8 minutes and it is harmless. Drying the chickpeas properly and removing loose skins cuts it down a lot. Popping chickpeas can jump against the heating element, so check under the crisper plate when you clean up; a small burnt chickpea there will smoke the next time you cook. Our guide on how to clean an air fryer covers getting at that space safely.

Air fryer chickpeas seasoning ideas

Each blend is for one 400g can. Add it with the oil after the first 10 minutes, never at the start.

Blend What goes in
Za'atar and sumac 1½ tsp za'atar, ½ tsp sumac, ½ tsp salt. Add a squeeze of lemon after cooking.
Baharat 1 tsp baharat, ½ tsp salt, pinch of chilli flakes
Chaat masala 1 tsp chaat masala, ¼ tsp chilli powder, salt to taste (chaat masala is already salty)
Lime and chilli ½ tsp chilli powder, ½ tsp salt, finely grated zest of 1 lime after cooking
Garlic and parmesan ½ tsp garlic powder, ½ tsp salt; toss with 2 tbsp finely grated parmesan as soon as they come out (choose a microbial-rennet hard cheese if you need it halal-certified)
Cinnamon sugar Swap the olive oil for melted coconut oil; toss with 1 tbsp sugar and ½ tsp cinnamon after cooking, not before, because sugar burns at 200°C

Sugar and cheese go on after cooking. Sugar blackens within a couple of minutes at this heat, and grated cheese melts into the basket mesh. For more ideas on what to roast alongside, see our time and temperature chart for vegetables.

Air Fryer Chickpeas

Scale:

Ingredients

Chickpeas

  • 1 can canned chickpeas (400g (14 oz) can, about 240g (8½ oz) drained)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (or any neutral oil)
  • ½ tsp fine salt (fine salt sticks better than flaky salt)

Smoky Cumin Seasoning

  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • ½ tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • 1 pinch cayenne pepper or chilli powder (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1

    Drain the chickpeas into a sieve and rinse under cold water until the foamy liquid (aquafaba) is gone. Shake the sieve hard for 20 seconds to throw off as much water as possible.

    Save the aquafaba in a jar in the fridge if you like. It whips like egg white for meringues and mousse.

  2. 2

    Tip the chickpeas onto a clean tea towel, fold it over and roll them gently under your palms for 30 seconds. Loose skins will slip off; pick them out and discard them. Leave the chickpeas spread out on the towel for 10 minutes while the air fryer heats.

    You do not have to peel every chickpea. Removing the loose skins is enough, because loose skins trap steam and burn before the chickpea is crisp.

  3. 3

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F) for 3 minutes. Mix the smoked paprika, cumin, garlic powder, salt and cayenne in a small bowl.

  4. 4

    Tip the dry, unoiled chickpeas into the basket in a single layer and air fry for 10 minutes, shaking the basket at the 5-minute mark. They will look pale and slightly wrinkled, and a few may pop. That is steam escaping and it is normal.

  5. 5

    Transfer the hot chickpeas to a heatproof bowl, drizzle with the oil and toss until every one is glossy. Sprinkle over the spice mix and toss again until evenly coated.

    Oiling hot chickpeas means the oil coats a dry surface instead of beading on a wet one, so the spices stick and the crust browns evenly.

  6. 6

    Return the chickpeas to the basket and air fry for 5 to 8 minutes more, shaking once. Pull them when they are a deep golden brown and rattle loudly when you shake the basket.

  7. 7

    Break one chickpea in half. The inside should be pale, dry and crumbly. If it is still soft and creamy in the very centre, give the batch 2 more minutes.

  8. 8

    Spread the chickpeas on a plate and let them cool for 5 minutes before eating. They crisp further as the last of the steam escapes.

Equipment

Air fryer · Sieve · Clean tea towel · Heatproof mixing bowl

Tips

  • Weigh the drained chickpeas once. If you have much more than 250g (9 oz), you have more than one layer's worth for a standard basket, so split the batch.
  • Shake the basket, do not stir with a spoon. Shaking turns the chickpeas over without knocking off the spices.
  • Use fine salt. Flaky salt bounces off a round chickpea and ends up in the bottom of the basket.
  • If your air fryer runs hot or has a small 3 to 4 litre basket, check at 13 minutes total. Compact baskets put the food closer to the element.
  • Cooked chickpeas from a jar are often softer than canned ones. Add 2 to 3 minutes to the plain first stage.

Troubleshooting

  • Crunchy outside, soft inside: the outside browned before the centre dried. Start them plain, as in this method, or drop to 180°C (350°F) and give them longer.
  • Spices taste bitter or burnt: add them for the last 5 to 8 minutes only. Paprika and garlic powder scorch if they sit at 200°C from the start.
  • Spices fall off: toss the chickpeas with oil while they are hot and dry, then add the spices. Oil on wet chickpeas beads up and the seasoning slides off with it.
  • Chickpeas went chewy overnight: they were put away warm or sealed in. Re-crisp at 180°C (350°F) for 3 to 4 minutes.
  • Some burnt, others pale: too many in the basket, or not shaken enough. One can per batch, shaken every 5 minutes.

Substitutions

  • No smoked paprika? Use sweet paprika plus a pinch of ground cumin for warmth.
  • Avocado or sunflower oil works in place of olive oil and handles the heat slightly better.
  • Oil-free: skip the oil and mist the chickpeas with a little lemon juice or aquafaba before adding the spices. They will be crunchy but the spices will not cling as well.
  • Chickpeas you have soaked and boiled yourself work the same way. Cook them until just tender, not soft, so they hold their shape.

Variations

  • Iftar snack bowl: toss with za'atar and sumac and serve alongside dates and a jug of laban.
  • Salad crunch: cook only 11 to 13 minutes so the centres stay a little tender, then scatter warm over fattoush in place of, or as well as, the fried bread.
  • Spicy honey: after cooking, toss in 1 tsp honey warmed with a pinch of chilli flakes, spread on baking paper and let set for 10 minutes.
  • Warm cauliflower and chickpea salad: roast the chickpeas first, then roast bite-size cauliflower florets at 200°C (400°F) for 13 to 15 minutes and toss the two together with tahini and lemon. Cooked in one basket they crowd each other, and the chickpeas need longer anyway.

What to Serve With It

  • As a topping for lentil soup, tomato soup or shorba in place of croutons.
  • Over a bowl of rice, roasted vegetables and a tahini-lemon dressing.
  • In a wrap with shredded lettuce, cucumber and garlic yoghurt.
  • On their own with a cup of karak chai in the afternoon.

Storage

Cool the chickpeas completely on an open plate, then store them at room temperature in a jar with the lid resting on top rather than screwed down, for up to 2 days. Sealing them warm or refrigerating them makes them chewy. They are crunchiest on the day they are made.

Reheating

Re-crisp in the air fryer at 180°C (350°F) for 3 to 4 minutes, shaking once. Let them cool for 2 minutes before eating. Do not microwave them; they turn soft and tough.

Make Ahead

Rinse, dry and peel the chickpeas up to a day ahead and keep them uncovered in the fridge on a towel-lined plate. The fridge air dries the surface further, which helps the crunch. Add the oil and spices only when you cook.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you cook chickpeas in an air fryer?

Cook canned chickpeas at 200°C (400°F) for 15 to 18 minutes in total, shaking every 5 minutes. For a softer centre to use on salads, stop at 11 to 13 minutes. For an extra-crunchy batch that keeps longer, cook at 180°C (350°F) for 22 to 25 minutes.

Do you need to remove the skins from chickpeas before air frying?

No, but removing the loose ones helps. Rolling the chickpeas in a towel slips off most skins in 30 seconds. Loose skins trap moisture and char before the chickpea is crisp, while skins still attached crisp up fine.

Can you put dried chickpeas straight into the air fryer?

No. Raw dried chickpeas must be soaked and boiled first. Air frying them raw leaves them rock hard, impossible to chew and hard to digest. Soak overnight, boil until just tender, drain and dry well, then follow the same method as canned.

Why are my chickpeas popping in the air fryer?

Water under the skin turns to steam and bursts it. A few pops in the first minutes is normal and harmless. Drying the chickpeas thoroughly and removing loose skins reduces popping. Check under the crisper plate afterwards for any that landed near the element.

How do you store air fryer chickpeas so they stay crispy?

Cool them completely on an open plate, then keep them at room temperature in a jar with the lid loosely resting on top for up to 2 days. Do not refrigerate them. If they soften, re-crisp at 180°C (350°F) for 3 to 4 minutes.

Are air fryer chickpeas healthy?

They are a reasonable snack. Chickpeas provide fibre and plant protein, and this recipe uses one tablespoon of oil for a whole can, far less than deep frying. Watch the salt if you are cooking them for children or eating a large bowl.

Can you air fry chickpeas without oil?

Yes. They still turn crunchy, though they brown less and the spices do not stick as well. Mist them with lemon juice or a little aquafaba after the first 10 minutes so the seasoning has something to cling to.
