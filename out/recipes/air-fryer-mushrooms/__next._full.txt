2f:T406,Mushrooms are about 90 percent water, and that is what decides how they turn out in an air fryer. In the first few minutes they release a lot of liquid. If the basket is crowded or the mushrooms were salted, that liquid has nowhere to go and they simmer in it, turning grey and rubbery. With space around them and no salt yet, the moving air carries the water off quickly, and only then does the surface start to brown.

This recipe tosses the mushrooms in plain oil, roasts them for 8 minutes, and only then adds butter, fresh garlic and salt for a final 2 to 3 minutes. Fresh garlic needs about that long to soften and turn pale gold. Put it in at the start and it is black and bitter before the mushrooms are done.

Serve them with [garlic butter steak bites](/recipes/air-fryer-steak-bites/), spoon them over toast, or pile them next to a pan of [Gulf shakshuka](/recipes/gulf-shakshuka/) at breakfast. For a fuller side, roast them with [air fryer onions](/recipes/air-fryer-onions/) using the timing in the variations below.30:T45a,Sirloin is the best all-round choice: tender, beefy and cheaper than ribeye. Use any steak you would happily grill whole.

- **Sirloin.** Labelled "top sirloin" on American beef, "rump" on British and Australian beef. Lean, with enough flavour to stand up to garlic butter.
- **Striploin (New York strip).** A fat cap down one side; trim it before cubing or it stays chewy.
- **Ribeye.** The juiciest option. The fat marbling melts at 200°C, so it forgives an extra minute.
- **Tenderloin (fillet).** Very tender, but mild and expensive. Cut bigger cubes, 4cm, and keep it rare to medium.

Avoid stewing beef, chuck and pre-cut "beef cubes". They're full of connective tissue that only softens after an hour or more of slow cooking, so a few minutes in the air fryer leaves them tough.

Much of the fresh beef in Gulf supermarkets is imported and halal-certified, and the labels follow the exporting country's naming, so the same sirloin cut can be sold as "rump steak" in one shop and "top sirloin" in the next. If you're unsure, ask the butcher counter for a steak at least 3cm thick so the cubes come out even.31:T421,For 2.5cm cubes at 200°C (400°F), allow 5 to 6 minutes for medium-rare, 6 to 8 minutes for medium and 9 to 11 minutes for well done, shaking once at 4 minutes. Bigger 4cm cubes add about 2 minutes to each stage.

Those times assume a preheated basket and a single layer. A crowded basket cooks the steak in its own steam: the juices pool in the gaps, the surface stays grey and the inside overcooks while you wait for colour. If you see liquid collecting around the cubes after the shake, the batch was too big.

Small cubes carry very little heat over once they leave the basket, usually 1 to 2°C, so pull them at the temperature you want to eat. USDA guidance for whole cuts of beef is 63°C (145°F) followed by a 3-minute rest. Rare and medium-rare are below that and are a personal choice; serve medium or above to pregnant guests, young children and anyone with a weakened immune system.

Without a thermometer, press a cube with tongs. Medium-rare still yields, medium springs back, and well done feels firm.
