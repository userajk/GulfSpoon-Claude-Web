30:T45a,Sirloin is the best all-round choice: tender, beefy and cheaper than ribeye. Use any steak you would happily grill whole.

- **Sirloin.** Labelled "top sirloin" on American beef, "rump" on British and Australian beef. Lean, with enough flavour to stand up to garlic butter.
- **Striploin (New York strip).** A fat cap down one side; trim it before cubing or it stays chewy.
- **Ribeye.** The juiciest option. The fat marbling melts at 200°C, so it forgives an extra minute.
- **Tenderloin (fillet).** Very tender, but mild and expensive. Cut bigger cubes, 4cm, and keep it rare to medium.

Avoid stewing beef, chuck and pre-cut "beef cubes". They're full of connective tissue that only softens after an hour or more of slow cooking, so a few minutes in the air fryer leaves them tough.

Much of the fresh beef in Gulf supermarkets is imported and halal-certified, and the labels follow the exporting country's naming, so the same sirloin cut can be sold as "rump steak" in one shop and "top sirloin" in the next. If you're unsure, ask the butcher counter for a steak at least 3cm thick so the cubes come out even.31:T421,For 2.5cm cubes at 200°C (400°F), allow 5 to 6 minutes for medium-rare, 6 to 8 minutes for medium and 9 to 11 minutes for well done, shaking once at 4 minutes. Bigger 4cm cubes add about 2 minutes to each stage.

Those times assume a preheated basket and a single layer. A crowded basket cooks the steak in its own steam: the juices pool in the gaps, the surface stays grey and the inside overcooks while you wait for colour. If you see liquid collecting around the cubes after the shake, the batch was too big.

Small cubes carry very little heat over once they leave the basket, usually 1 to 2°C, so pull them at the temperature you want to eat. USDA guidance for whole cuts of beef is 63°C (145°F) followed by a 3-minute rest. Rare and medium-rare are below that and are a personal choice; serve medium or above to pregnant guests, young children and anyone with a weakened immune system.

Without a thermometer, press a cube with tongs. Medium-rare still yields, medium springs back, and well done feels firm. If all you have is large potatoes, cut them into even 2.5cm cubes and follow the same method: rinse, dry, oil, season, shake. Spend a minute sorting them before you cut: leave the smallest whole, halve the middle sizes and quarter the biggest, so every piece is about the same thickness. For a sweeter side in the same basket, try [diced sweet potato with smoked paprika or cinnamon](/recipes/air-fryer-sweet-potato-cubes/).33:T4f4,Halved baby potatoes take 15 to 18 minutes at 200°C (400°F), and 2.5cm cubes of large potatoes take 18 to 22 minutes. Whole baby potatoes take longer, 20 to 25 minutes, because the skin slows moisture loss and heat has further to travel to the centre. That is also why whole ones come out creamy and soft while halves come out crisp.

Size matters more than variety. Every extra centimetre adds roughly 5 minutes. Baby gold potatoes are slightly drier and brown a touch faster; baby red potatoes are waxier and need a minute or two more before they feel tender.

Load is the other variable. Halves need a single layer. Cubes cope with two layers if you shake hard. Fill the basket to the brim and the top layer shields the bottom one, so you get pale, steamed potatoes underneath even after 30 minutes. For six people, two batches are faster in total than one crammed basket. Oven-style air fryers with trays sit the food further from the element, so add 3 to 5 minutes and swap the trays halfway.

For dinner from one machine, cook the potatoes first and keep them warm on a tray in a low oven, then use the hot basket for [crispy-skinned air fryer chicken thighs](/recipes/air-fryer-chicken-thighs/) or [salmon fillets with crisp skin](/recipes/air-fryer-salmon/).34:T43f,Any of them will cook well. They differ in texture, and in how well they survive being shaken.

- **Baby potatoes (gold, red or purple):** young, waxy and thin-skinned. Creamy inside and crisp on the cut face, with no peeling. The least work and the most reliable batch.
- **Yukon Gold and other all-rounders:** medium starch with a buttery interior. Cubes hold their shape, and the thin skin can stay on.
- **Russet and other floury potatoes (Maris Piper, King Edward):** high starch, low moisture. Cubes roughen and crisp hard on the outside and turn fluffy inside, but the corners can break when shaken. The broken bits go extra crunchy.
- **Large red-skinned and other waxy potatoes:** hold their shape but brown less and stay dense inside when cubed.

Loose potatoes in Gulf supermarkets are often labelled by country of origin rather than variety. To check, cut one in half and rub the faces together. If they stick and leave a white, starchy film, the potato is floury and will crisp well as cubes. This recipe tosses the mushrooms in plain oil, roasts them for 8 minutes, and only then adds butter, fresh garlic and salt for a final 2 to 3 minutes. Fresh garlic needs about that long to soften and turn pale gold. Put it in at the start and it is black and bitter before the mushrooms are done.

Breast meat dries out. Wings are fiddly. Thighs just work.

The key to perfect air fryer chicken thighs is dry skin. Moisture is the enemy of crispness, so a thorough pat-down with paper towels before seasoning is non-negotiable. After that, a simple rub of oil, garlic powder, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper is all you need. In Gulf kitchens, chicken thighs are a weeknight staple. The air fryer has made them even more practical: no preheating a full oven, no splatter from a frying pan, and dinner on the table in 25 minutes.

If you&#39;re unsure, ask the butcher counter for a steak at least 3cm thick so the cubes come out even.</p>
39:T43b,<p>For 2.5cm cubes at 200°C (400°F), allow 5 to 6 minutes for medium-rare, 6 to 8 minutes for medium and 9 to 11 minutes for well done, shaking once at 4 minutes. Bigger 4cm cubes add about 2 minutes to each stage.</p>
<p>Those times assume a preheated basket and a single layer. A crowded basket cooks the steak in its own steam: the juices pool in the gaps, the surface stays grey and the inside overcooks while you wait for colour. If you see liquid collecting around the cubes after the shake, the batch was too big.</p>
<p>Small cubes carry very little heat over once they leave the basket, usually 1 to 2°C, so pull them at the temperature you want to eat. USDA guidance for whole cuts of beef is 63°C (145°F) followed by a 3-minute rest. Rare and medium-rare are below that and are a personal choice; serve medium or above to pregnant guests, young children and anyone with a weakened immune system.</p>
<p>Without a thermometer, press a cube with tongs. Medium-rare still yields, medium springs back, and well done feels firm. If in doubt, cut the largest one open.</p>
