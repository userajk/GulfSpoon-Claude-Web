Air Fryer Steak Bites with Garlic Butter

By Omar Hussain|Updated: November 19, 2026
Prep 10 mins
Cook 8 mins
Total 21 mins
Servings 4
Difficulty Easy
Cuisine International

Bite-sized cubes of halal sirloin seared in a very hot air fryer, then tossed in garlic and parsley butter. They take under 10 minutes to cook, and the doneness chart below tells you exactly when to pull them for rare through to well done.

Quick answer

Cook 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes of sirloin in a preheated air fryer at 200°C (400°F) for 6 to 8 minutes, shaking once at 4 minutes, until browned on the edges and 63°C (145°F) inside for medium, the USDA minimum for beef. Rest them for 3 minutes, then toss in melted garlic butter. Medium-rare (5 to 6 minutes) sits below that minimum.

Steak bites suit the air fryer better than a whole steak does. A thick steak has one top and one bottom, and the side resting on the basket barely browns. Cubes expose five or six faces to the fan, and a quick shake turns them all, so you get a browned crust on most of the surface in the time it takes to cook the inside to medium.

Start with a grilling cut such as sirloin, striploin or ribeye; stewing beef stays tough after a few minutes of high heat. Cut the cubes evenly to 2.5cm, which is big enough for the outside to brown before the centre overcooks. Then use a thermometer for the last minute, when a single cube can go from pink to grey.

The garlic butter goes on after cooking. Butter in the basket at 200°C burns, and minced garlic turns bitter in about a minute at that heat, so you melt them together separately and toss the hot steak through.

What is the best cut of steak for air fryer steak bites?

Sirloin is the best all-round choice: tender, beefy and cheaper than ribeye. Use any steak you would happily grill whole.

  • Sirloin. Labelled "top sirloin" on American beef, "rump" on British and Australian beef. Lean, with enough flavour to stand up to garlic butter.
  • Striploin (New York strip). A fat cap down one side; trim it before cubing or it stays chewy.
  • Ribeye. The juiciest option. The fat marbling melts at 200°C, so it forgives an extra minute.
  • Tenderloin (fillet). Very tender, but mild and expensive. Cut bigger cubes, 4cm, and keep it rare to medium.

Avoid stewing beef, chuck and pre-cut "beef cubes". They're full of connective tissue that only softens after an hour or more of slow cooking, so a few minutes in the air fryer leaves them tough.

Much of the fresh beef in Gulf supermarkets is imported and halal-certified, and the labels follow the exporting country's naming, so the same sirloin cut can be sold as "rump steak" in one shop and "top sirloin" in the next. If you're unsure, ask the butcher counter for a steak at least 3cm thick so the cubes come out even.

How long to cook steak bites in the air fryer

For 2.5cm cubes at 200°C (400°F), allow 5 to 6 minutes for medium-rare, 6 to 8 minutes for medium and 9 to 11 minutes for well done, shaking once at 4 minutes. Bigger 4cm cubes add about 2 minutes to each stage.

Those times assume a preheated basket and a single layer. A crowded basket cooks the steak in its own steam: the juices pool in the gaps, the surface stays grey and the inside overcooks while you wait for colour. If you see liquid collecting around the cubes after the shake, the batch was too big.

Small cubes carry very little heat over once they leave the basket, usually 1 to 2°C, so pull them at the temperature you want to eat. USDA guidance for whole cuts of beef is 63°C (145°F) followed by a 3-minute rest. Rare and medium-rare are below that and are a personal choice; serve medium or above to pregnant guests, young children and anyone with a weakened immune system.

Without a thermometer, press a cube with tongs. Medium-rare still yields, medium springs back, and well done feels firm. If in doubt, cut the largest one open.

Steak bites and potatoes in one basket

To make a full meal in the air fryer, cook the potatoes first. Cut 450g (1 lb) of potatoes into 2cm cubes or halve small new potatoes, toss with 1 tbsp oil and ½ tsp salt and air fry at 200°C (400°F) for 15 minutes, shaking twice. Push them to the edges of the basket, add the seasoned steak in the middle and cook for another 6 to 8 minutes for medium. The potatoes finish in the beef juices.

Our crispy cubed potatoes page has the full method and a time chart for every cut. Garlic mushrooms also go well: add halved button mushrooms with the steak and they'll be browned by the time it reaches medium.

Can you cook steak bites from frozen?

Yes, though the crust is lighter than with fresh steak. Freeze raw cubes spread out on a lined tray until solid, then bag them so they don't stick together. Cook from frozen at 200°C (400°F) for 4 minutes with just a light coat of oil, then shake, add the salt and spices (they slide off a frozen surface) and cook for another 6 to 9 minutes for medium. Check the largest cube with a thermometer, because the centre lags behind the outside more than with fresh meat.

Don't freeze cubes that have already been tossed in garlic butter raw; the garlic goes harsh and the butter stops the surface browning.

Air Fryer Garlic Butter Steak Bites

Scale:

Ingredients

Steak Bites

  • 700 g halal beef sirloin, striploin or ribeye (1½ lb, about 3cm (1¼ inch) thick, trimmed and cut into 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil or other high-heat oil
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper (freshly ground)
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika

Garlic Butter

  • 40 g butter (3 tbsp, salted or unsalted)
  • 3 cloves garlic (finely grated or minced)
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley (finely chopped)
  • 1 pinch chilli flakes (optional)
  • 1 tsp lemon juice

Instructions

  1. 1

    Trim any thick fat or gristle from the steak and cut it into even 2.5cm (1 inch) cubes. Pat the cubes very dry with paper towels.

    Even sizing matters more than the exact size. A mix of 2cm and 4cm pieces will give you some overcooked and some raw.

  2. 2

    Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F) for 5 minutes. A properly hot basket starts browning the steak on contact.

  3. 3

    While it heats, toss the cubes in a bowl with the oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder and smoked paprika until evenly coated.

    Season just before cooking. Salt left on the meat for 10 to 30 minutes pulls moisture to the surface, which slows browning.

  4. 4

    Spread the cubes in the basket in a single layer with small gaps between them. In a 5–6 litre basket, 700g usually fits in one batch; if the cubes are touching on all sides, cook in two batches.

  5. 5

    Air fry for 4 minutes, then shake the basket hard or turn the cubes with tongs so the pale sides face up.

  6. 6

    Cook for another 1 to 4 minutes depending on how you like it (see the chart). Check the largest cube with an instant-read thermometer: 63°C (145°F) for medium, the USDA minimum for beef, or 57°C (135°F) if you choose medium-rare.

    A second batch cooks about 1 minute faster because the basket is already hot.

  7. 7

    Tip the steak into a warm bowl and rest for 3 minutes. Meanwhile, melt the butter with the garlic in a small pan over medium-low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until it sizzles and smells of garlic but the garlic is still pale. Stir in the chilli flakes, parsley and lemon juice.

  8. 8

    Pour the garlic butter over the rested steak, add any juices from the bowl, toss well and serve straight away.

Equipment

Air fryer (5–6 litre basket) · Instant-read thermometer · Sharp knife and board · Small saucepan · Tongs

Tips

  • Cut the steak while it's fridge-cold. Firm beef is easier to cube evenly.
  • Shake hard at the halfway point. Cubes that stay on the same face brown on one side only.
  • Use a high-heat oil such as sunflower, avocado or light olive oil. Extra virgin olive oil works but can smoke at 200°C.
  • Rest the steak in a warm bowl, not on a board, so the juices stay with the meat and end up in the butter sauce.
  • If your air fryer only reaches 190°C, add 1 to 2 minutes and expect a little less browning.

Troubleshooting

  • Steak is grey, not browned: the basket was crowded or not preheated, or the meat was wet. Pat dry, preheat for 5 minutes and cook in batches.
  • Bites are chewy: the cut was a stewing cut or the cubes were overcooked. Use sirloin, striploin or ribeye and pull at the target temperature.
  • Some cubes are overdone, others underdone: uneven cutting. Aim for 2.5cm on every side and trim thin tail pieces off for another use.
  • Garlic butter tastes bitter: the garlic browned. Melt over medium-low heat and take it off as soon as it sizzles.
  • Lots of smoke: fat dripping onto a dirty drawer. Wipe out old grease before cooking and trim thick fat from the steak.

Substitutions

  • No fresh garlic? Use ½ tsp garlic powder in the butter, added off the heat.
  • Dairy-free: replace the butter with 3 tbsp olive oil warmed gently with the garlic.
  • Lamb leg steak or boneless lamb shoulder steak, cut into 2.5cm cubes, cooks in the same time.
  • For a deeper, steak-house savouriness, add 1 tsp halal soy sauce and ½ tsp lemon juice to the seasoning.

Variations

  • Shawarma-spiced: season with 1 tsp seven spice (baharat), ½ tsp cumin and ½ tsp sumac, and serve with garlic toum and flatbread.
  • Chilli-lime: swap the parsley and lemon for coriander and lime, and add a sliced green chilli to the butter.
  • Herb sauce: skip the butter and spoon over a quick green sauce of parsley, coriander, garlic, olive oil, lemon juice and chilli flakes.
  • Steak and pepper skewers: thread cubes onto short wooden skewers with pepper chunks and cook for the same time, turning once.

What to Serve With It

  • Serve over basmati rice with the garlic butter spooned on top.
  • Pile into warm flatbread with shredded lettuce, tomato and garlic yoghurt.
  • Pair with roasted asparagus or green beans for a low-carb plate.
  • Serve as a sharing plate with cocktail sticks and the butter in a small bowl for dipping.

Storage

Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container within 2 hours of cooking. They keep for 3 to 4 days. Cooked steak bites can be frozen for up to 2 months, though they lose some juiciness.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 180°C (350°F) for 2 to 3 minutes, shaking once, until just warmed through. Any longer and medium-rare steak turns well done. From frozen, thaw overnight in the fridge first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you cook steak bites in the air fryer?

Cook 2.5cm (1 inch) steak bites at 200°C (400°F) for 6 to 8 minutes for medium (63°C/145°F, the USDA minimum) and 9 to 11 minutes for well done, or 5 to 6 minutes if you prefer medium-rare. Shake the basket once at 4 minutes. Larger 4cm cubes need about 2 minutes more.

What temperature should I cook steak bites in the air fryer?

Use 200°C (400°F), the highest setting on most air fryers, and preheat for 5 minutes. High heat browns the outside of the cubes before the centre overcooks. At lower temperatures the steak cooks through before it develops colour.

Do you have to flip steak bites in the air fryer?

Yes, give the basket a hard shake or turn the cubes with tongs halfway through. The faces touching the basket get far less airflow, so without a shake those sides stay grey while the rest browns.

Why are my air fryer steak bites tough?

Usually the cut or the cooking time is wrong. Stewing beef and chuck need slow cooking and stay tough after a few minutes of high heat. Use sirloin, striploin or ribeye, cut evenly, and pull the cubes at 63°C (145°F), the USDA minimum, or at 57°C (135°F) if you prefer medium-rare.

Should you marinate steak bites before air frying?

It isn't necessary with a tender cut. A dry seasoning of salt, pepper, garlic powder and paprika added just before cooking gives a better crust. If you do marinate, keep it under 2 hours and pat the cubes dry before they go in the basket.

Can you cook frozen steak bites in the air fryer?

Yes. Cook frozen cubes at 200°C (400°F) for 4 minutes with a little oil, shake, season, then cook another 6 to 9 minutes for medium. Check the largest cube reaches 63°C (145°F), as frozen centres lag behind the surface.

How do you reheat steak bites in the air fryer?

Reheat at 180°C (350°F) for 2 to 3 minutes, shaking once. Keep it short, because the small cubes heat through quickly and will go from pink to well done if left in longer.
