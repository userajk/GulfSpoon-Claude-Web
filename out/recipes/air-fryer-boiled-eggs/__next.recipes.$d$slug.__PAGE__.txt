33:T451,Use 135°C (275°F). An air fryer cooks with fast-moving dry air from an element just above the food (see [how an air fryer works](/beyond-the-recipes/how-does-an-air-fryer-work/)), so the shell sits in air well above the 100°C of boiling water. 135°C is low enough that the outside of the white stays tender while the heat works into the yolk, and high enough to finish a hard-boiled egg in about a quarter of an hour.

Going lower is fine. At 120°C (250°F) the eggs take 1 to 2 minutes longer at each stage and the whites are slightly softer. Going higher causes problems. At 150°C (300°F), a common setting because it is the lowest air-fry temperature on some models, hard-boiled eggs are ready in about 13 to 15 minutes, but the whites are firmer and brown spots are more likely where the shell touches the basket. At 180°C (350°F) or above, the shell can crack as the air inside expands, and the white near the shell turns rubbery before the yolk sets.
